## How to read the tables

### Difficulty (`Diff`)

| # | Level | What it means |
|:-:|---|---|
| **1** | Day one | Teachable in one class, usable that night. |
| **2** | Easy | A few weeks of drilling. Forgiving of mistakes. |
| **3** | Moderate | Needs timing, a setup, and a reaction to feed off. |
| **4** | Hard | Long setup chains, tight details, or specific attributes (flexibility, balance, clinch feel). |
| **5** | Expert | Specialist move. High failure cost. |

### How common (`Com`)

| # | Level | What it means |
|:-:|---|---|
| **5** | Everywhere | Seen every session. Non-optional. |
| **4** | Very common | Core of most people's games. |
| **3** | Common | Regular sighting. |
| **2** | Occasional | Situational or specialist. |
| **1** | Rare | Niche, elite-only, or ruleset-locked. |

**A note on honesty:** ratings are informed coaching judgment, not a peer-reviewed dataset. **Thai transliteration is not standardised** — the same word appears as *sok tat*, *sok tad* or *sok tath* depending on the source. Names here follow common gym usage, and where sources disagree on a name's exact meaning, this file says so rather than guess.

**Terms:** *mud* = punch · *tae* = kick · *teep* = push kick · *khao* = knee · *sok* = elbow · *chap kho* = clinch ("grab the neck") · *nak muay* = fighter · *kru* = teacher/trainer.

**Stance convention:** orthodox (left foot forward) unless stated. "Lead" = forward limb, "rear" = back limb.

## Part I — The Map

Muay Thai uses **eight points of contact** — two fists, two elbows, two knees, two shins/feet — across four ranges, and it is the only major striking art here in which the *clinch* is a central, scoring part of the fight.

| Range | Distance | Primary tools | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Long** | Only kicks reach | Teep, oblique/low kick, round kick | Where Thai fighters score with kicks and teeps |
| **Mid** | Punches reach | Jab, cross, hooks, kicks, switch-kicks | Combinations, mixed with kicks |
| **Short** | Elbows, knees, hooks reach | Elbows, knees, uppercuts | The most dangerous range |
| **Clinch** | Chest-to-chest, neck control | Knees, elbows, sweeps, dumps | A fight in itself |

**The core rules of the sport:**
1. *Posture is power.* The fighter with the better posture wins the clinch; the fighter with the worse posture gets kneed.
2. *The teep is the jab of Muay Thai.* It manages distance, interrupts rhythm and scores.
3. *Check with the shin and counter immediately.* Defending a kick without a return scores nothing.
4. *Balance wins.* A fighter who stays on both feet while the opponent loses balance scores a sweep, and a sweep can score even if the opponent lands a strike.
5. *Strikes that show control and balance score; wild volume doesn't.* See Part XIII.

## Part II — Stance, Guard and Posture

| Stance / guard | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Muay Thai stance (square-ish, upright)** | Upright, hips fairly square, weight back (approximately 60% on the rear leg), lead heel up, lead leg light so it can teep or check. Chin tucked. | 1 | 5 |
| **Long guard** | Lead hand extended, rear hand at the chin; shields and measures. | 2 | 4 |
| **High guard (kick-block guard)** | Both hands high and forearms vertical; the elbows tuck in against the ribs to defend body kicks. | 1 | 5 |
| **Lead-hand-out guard** | Lead hand extended, ready to touch or push; set up the teep. | 2 | 4 |
| **Southpaw** | Mirror stance; southpaw's lead leg is outside the orthodox lead leg, changing every kick and teep. | 2 | 4 |
| **Switch stance** | Changing stance mid-fight to throw power from either side. | 4 | 3 |
| **Bladed stance (power striker)** | A more side-on stance used by fighters with strong kicks. | 3 | 3 |
| **Square stance (clinch posture)** | Square hips, lower, wider base; used in the clinch to stay stable. | 2 | 5 |
| **Clinch posture** | Chin tucked, spine straight, hips under shoulders, head not forced below the opponent's. The most important posture to keep in the clinch. | 3 | 5 |

**Weight distribution:** about 60% on the rear leg for kicking range, shifted forward when closing to clinch.

| Element | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|

| **Pra jiad** | Armbands worn on the biceps, traditionally containing amulets; removed before the fight. | 1 | 4 |
| **Sarama music (Muay music)** | The live music — a double-reed pi and drums — that sets the pace of the fight; the tempo rises with the action. | 1 | 5 |
| **Muay Boran** | The ancient, pre-ring forms of Muay Thai (including regional styles); the origin of many of today's techniques. | — | 2 |
| **Kru–student relationship** | Respect and gym loyalty; the kru (and the gym's name) is part of a fighter's identity. | — | 5 |
| **Gym culture in Thailand** | Training twice a day, camp-based, fight-oriented. | — | 5 |

## Part IV — Footwork

| Movement | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Bounce / rhythmic step** | Small rhythmic rocking that keeps weight loaded and hides the teep. | 1 | 5 |
| **Step-and-drag** | Advance and retreat in stance. | 1 | 5 |
| **Circling** | Lateral movement away from the opponent's rear leg (the power side). | 2 | 5 |
| **Pivot** | Rotate on the lead foot to open an angle; also the base of every round kick. | 3 | 5 |

| **Hop in to clinch** | A quick entry, protected by a hand or a teep, to grab the head. | 3 | 4 |
| **Lead-leg lift (check readiness)** | Keeping the lead knee slightly raised to be ready for the check. | 2 | 5 |
| **Reset step** | Step out of the clinch or the exchange to re-establish the range. | 2 | 5 |

## Part V — Punches (Mud)

Muay Thai's punches are the same shapes as in **[boxing.md](boxing.md)** but are thrown *with the other weapons in mind*: higher guard, more upright posture, less head movement and fewer deep slips (because of knees and kicks).

| Punch | Muay Thai adjustment | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Jab** | Often thrown to blind and set up the teep or kick; a pawing jab hides intent. | 1 | 5 |
| **Cross** | Thrown with a pivot and a stable base; commonly follows a teep. | 1 | 5 |
| **Lead hook** | Used to draw the guard before a kick or elbow. | 2 | 4 |
| **Rear hook** | Less common; often replaced by an elbow at that range. | 3 | 3 |
| **Uppercut** | Short, in close — sets up a knee or elbow. | 3 | 3 |
| **Overhand** | Used to set up the clinch entry. | 3 | 3 |
| **Body punch** | Used to drop the guard before a kick or head strike. | 3 | 3 |
| **Superman punch** | A jump into a straight punch. | 4 | 1 |
| **Spinning backfist** | Legal; thrown sparingly. | 4 | 1 |
| **Hammer fist** | Legal in some rules; not typical. | 3 | 1 |

**Note:** in Thai stadium scoring, punches are generally given less weight than kicks, knees and elbows (see Part XIII).

## Part VI — Teeps (Push Kicks)

The teep is the signature distance tool. Done well it is a **non-scoring defence and a scoring attack at once**.

| Teep | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Rear teep (straight, to the body)** | Knee up, push the ball of the foot or heel into the belly or sternum; hips thrust forward. Stops an advance and scores in Thai-style judging. | 2 | 5 |
| **Lead teep (jab teep)** | Lead-leg push; fast, a distance-keeper and rhythm-breaker. | 2 | 5 |
| **Teep to the face** | The push aimed at the chin; humiliating, and scores. | 3 | 3 |
| **Teep to the thigh (leg teep)** | Pushing the lead thigh to unbalance the opponent. | 2 | 4 |
| **Teep to the knee (oblique/low teep)** | Teep at the front of the lead knee with a turned-out toe. Controls the lead leg. | 3 | 3 |
| **Side teep (side kick)** | A sideways push with the heel. | 3 | 3 |
| **Switch teep** | A teep after a switch step; the opponent doesn't expect it. | 3 | 3 |
| **Jumping teep** | A teep with a hop for range or surprise. | 4 | 1 |
| **Spinning back teep (back kick)** | Spin, strike with the heel to the body. | 4 | 2 |
| **Teep and step** | Teep followed immediately by a stepping combination. | 3 | 4 |
| **Teep as a rhythm-setter** | Repeated teeps to keep the opponent from setting. | 2 | 5 |

## Part VII — Kicks (Tae)

Muay Thai round kicks hit with the **shin**, not the foot, and use a full hip turn rather than a snap. The standing leg pivots so the heel points away from the target.

| Kick | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Rear body kick (roundhouse)** | Pivot, swing the whole leg through; shin hits the ribs, liver or flank. The primary scoring kick. | 2 | 5 |
| **Rear low kick** | The same to the thigh (outer) or calf. | 2 | 5 |
| **Rear head kick** | Round kick to the neck or head. | 3 | 4 |
| **Lead-leg kick (low, body, head)** | Faster, lighter; used against a forward-leaning opponent. | 3 | 4 |
| **Switch kick** | Switch step then kick with the new rear leg; surprise power. | 3 | 5 |
| **Calf kick** | A low kick aimed at the calf; common in modern Muay Thai. | 2 | 4 |
| **Inside leg kick** | Targets the inner thigh of the lead leg. | 3 | 3 |
| **Diagonal (angled-down) kick** | A kick that travels downward at an angle to the neck or collarbone. Seen in traditional styles. | 3 | 3 |
| **Axe kick (heel drop)** | A raised leg dropping the heel. | 4 | 1 |
| **Spinning heel kick / hook kick** | A spin to land with the heel. | 5 | 1 |
| **Question mark kick** | A front-curving kick to the head. | 4 | 2 |
| **Jumping kick** | A kick from a jump. | 5 | 1 |
| **Step-up kick** | Step the rear foot forward and kick with the new rear leg, to cover range and add power. | 3 | 3 |
| **Kick from the clinch (disengage kick)** | A fast kick as the clinch breaks. | 3 | 3 |
| **Catch-and-kick** | After catching the opponent's kick, a counter kick to the base leg. | 3 | 4 |

**Targets:** head · neck · ribs and liver (body kicks) · solar plexus · thigh · calf · knee · foot. **The groin is illegal.**

## Part VIII — Knees (Khao)

Knees are driven by the **hip**, not by lifting the leg. They are legal on the head and body; some knee types need the clinch.

| Knee | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Straight knee (khao dtrong)** | Drive the knee forward and up into the belly or sternum. | 2 | 5 |
| **Curving knee (khao tat / khao chiang)** | A curved, diagonal knee into the ribs or flank, thrown from the clinch or at mid-range; hips swing across. | 3 | 4 |
| **Small knee (khao noi)** | A short knee in close, striking the thigh, hip or ribs; used to keep the opponent from posturing. | 3 | 4 |
| **Long knee (khao yao)** | A long, extended knee to the body at range. | 3 | 3 |
| **Flying knee (khao loi)** | Jump into a knee at the chin or chest. | 4 | 2 |
| **Jumping knee (khao kratot)** | A step-and-jump straight knee. | 4 | 2 |
| **Stepping knee** | A step forward and then the knee. | 3 | 4 |
| **Knee from the clinch to the head (against a bent opponent)** | Pull the head down into the knee. Legal in Muay Thai; illegal in many kickboxing rules. | 3 | 5 |
| **Switch knee** | Switch, then drive. | 3 | 3 |
| **Knee to the thigh (clinch)** | A short knee to the lead thigh to disrupt balance. | 2 | 4 |
| **Double knee (both legs, flying)** | Jump into a double knee. Showpiece. | 5 | 1 |

## Part IX — Elbows (Sok)

Elbows cut. They are legal in Muay Thai (not in K-1 or boxing) and are among the most powerful close-range tools. **Names vary between sources; treat the Thai name as a gym convention.**

| Elbow | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Horizontal elbow (sok tat)** | Swing the elbow in a flat arc, with a hip and shoulder rotation. | 3 | 5 |
| **Upward elbow (sok ngat / sok hud, depending on source)** | The elbow rises and strikes the chin from below. | 3 | 4 |
| **Diagonal (slanting) elbow (sok chiang)** | A diagonal downward elbow from high to low across the target. | 3 | 4 |
| **Downward elbow (sok sab / sok ti, depending on source)** | A downward chop onto the top of the head or the brow bone. | 3 | 4 |
| **Spear (straight) elbow (sok tong)** | A straight forward point-first strike. | 3 | 3 |
| **Double elbow (sok ku)** | Both elbows strike together; often a downward crush on a bent-over opponent. | 4 | 2 |
| **Spinning elbow (sok klab)** | Spin and strike with the back of the elbow. | 5 | 2 |
| **Reverse (back) elbow** | Elbow thrown backward over the shoulder as the fighter turns. | 4 | 2 |
| **Flying elbow** | Jump into a downward elbow. Showpiece. | 5 | 1 |
| **Counter elbow (in-close)** | Meet an advancing opponent with an elbow to the brow. | 4 | 4 |
| **Elbow off the clinch break** | An elbow as the opponent separates. | 3 | 3 |
| **Elbow to the arm or glove (blocking elbow)** | Elbows used to stop a kick or punch with the point. | 3 | 2 |

**Elbows and cuts:** elbows often cause cuts and stoppages. Judges and referees may stop a fight for a cut, and cuts alter scoring strategy.

## Part X — The Clinch (Chap Kho)

Clinching is a fight inside the fight. It scores — a dominant clinch with knees, sweeps and control earns points — and is where Thai fighters tire and break opponents down.

### X.a — Clinch holds

| Hold | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Double collar tie (plum / double neck tie)** | Both hands interlocked behind the head, elbows tight, forearms pressing the collarbones; the dominant clinch. | 3 | 5 |
| **Single collar tie** | One hand behind the head; the other free (for an elbow or to pull). | 2 | 4 |
| **Collar tie with an arm drag** | Pull the opponent's arm while controlling the head. | 3 | 3 |
| **Overhook (swim)** | An arm over the top of the opponent's arm. | 2 | 4 |
| **Underhook** | An arm under the opponent's arm to rotate them. | 2 | 4 |
| **Double underhooks (body lock)** | Both arms under; belly-to-belly. | 3 | 3 |
| **Side clinch (angled)** | Body positioned to the side with a head-and-arm control. | 3 | 4 |
| **Head control from behind** | Rear clinch used to knee or throw. | 4 | 2 |
| **Low clinch (waist grip)** | Grip around the waist; wrestling-style. | 3 | 2 |
| **Arm-trap clinch (pinning an arm)** | Hold one arm so only you can strike. | 3 | 3 |

### X.b — Clinch strikes and control

| Technique | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Knee from the double collar tie** | Pull the head down, drive the knee into ribs or the head. | 3 | 5 |
| **Elbow from the collar tie** | Elbow to the temple or brow while the other hand holds the neck. | 4 | 3 |
| **Head-turn and pull (off-balancing)** | Pull the head in a circle to break posture. | 3 | 5 |
| **Pummelling (inside position battle)** | Fight for the inside hands. | 2 | 5 |
| **Frame and push (pushing off)** | Frame at the chest or biceps to create space. | 2 | 5 |
| **Posture control (lifting the chin)** | Push the opponent's chin up or hold the head high to keep posture. | 3 | 5 |
| **Elbow-pressure (drive-through)** | Press the forearm into the neck to turn the head. | 3 | 4 |
| **Short punches in the clinch** | Hooks, uppercuts when the hands are free. | 2 | 3 |

### X.c — Sweeps, dumps and throws (clinch)

| Technique | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Pull-and-trip (basic sweep)** | Pull the head in a direction and kick the standing leg; the opponent falls. | 3 | 4 |
| **Leg-hook sweep** | Hook the opponent's leg from inside with your own and turn. | 3 | 4 |
| **Ankle-pick sweep** | Hand picks the ankle as the opponent steps. | 3 | 2 |
| **Catch-and-sweep (kick catch)** | Catch the round kick, then sweep the base leg. | 3 | 5 |
| **Teep-catch and dump** | Catch a teep, twist and put the opponent down. | 3 | 3 |
| **Clinch dump (hip-throw style)** | Off-balance the opponent and throw them to the canvas using the hips. | 4 | 3 |
| **Foot sweep** | Sweep the opponent's foot as it steps. | 3 | 3 |
| **Rotating dump (turn the head)** | Spin the opponent by the head into a fall. | 4 | 2 |
| **Rear-clinch throw** | A throw from behind. | 4 | 1 |

**Note on throws:** Muay Thai allows sweeps and throws that end with the opponent on the canvas, as long as the fighter doesn't slam in a dangerous way or throw with a joint lock. Rules differ by promotion, with some banning throws entirely.

### X.d — Clinch defense and escapes

| Defense | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Pre-clinch hand fighting** | Hands inside the opponent's elbows to deny the collar tie. | 3 | 5 |
| **Framing against the chest** | Forearms against the chest; prevent the lock. | 2 | 5 |
| **Elbow-through (break the grip)** | Drive the elbows between the opponent's forearms to break the tie. | 3 | 4 |
| **Underhook and turn** | Underhook and rotate to the side. | 3 | 4 |
| **Posture reset (head up)** | Keep the chin up and the spine straight to avoid knees. | 2 | 5 |
| **Knee defense (blocking)** | Knee or arm against the incoming knee. | 3 | 4 |
| **Push-off and strike** | Push and fire a teep or kick. | 3 | 4 |

### XI.a — Defending kicks and knees

| Defense | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Shin check (low and mid kick)** | Lift the lead (or rear) leg and meet the incoming shin with your own; the most important defense in Muay Thai. | 2 | 5 |

| **Step-back (range)** | Retreat out of reach. | 1 | 5 |

| **Knee block (clinch)** | Take the knee on the arms or thigh. | 2 | 5 |

## Part XIII — Styles, Strategy and Scoring Culture

### XIII.a — Traditional Thai fighting archetypes

Thai fans and trainers use these style names. **Treat them as archetypes, not rigid classes.**

| Style | What it is | Typical tools |
|---|---|---|
| **Muay femur (technical fighter)** | Balanced technician; clean, careful, scores with accuracy and timing; the most respected style. | Teep, kick, counter, balance |
| **Muay tae (kicker)** | Wins with kicks — power round kicks to the body and head. | Round kicks, teeps |
| **Muay khao (knee fighter)** | Fights in close with the knee. | Knees, clinch |
| **Muay mat (puncher)** | Strong hands; boxing-based. | Punches, hooks |
| **Muay sok (elbow fighter)** | Short-range elbow cutter. | Elbows, cuts |
| **Muay plam (clinch fighter)** | Wrestles in the clinch. | Clinch, sweeps, knees |
| **Counter fighter** | Waits for the opponent to commit, then counters. (A general archetype, not one of the classic Thai style names above.) | Counters, teeps |
| **Pressure fighter** | Moves forward continuously, leg kicks and body work. | Low kick, body kick, clinch |
| **Dutch / Western influenced** | Boxing-heavy; leg kicks and combinations; less Thai-style balance. | Combinations, low kicks |

### XIII.b — How scoring culture works (Thai stadiums)

Thai stadium judging (Lumpinee, Rajadamnern and others) differs from Western point scoring. *Verify against the venue's current practice — promotions differ.*

| Factor | General trend |
|---|---|
| **Kicks and knees vs punches** | Kicks, knees and elbows that land with power usually score more than punches. |
| **Balance and control** | A fighter who stays balanced and controls the ring beats one who is flailing. |
| **Damage** | Visible damage (the opponent being rocked or knocked down) matters. |
| **Later rounds** | Rounds 4 and 5 can carry greater weight in the overall impression — a fighter who closes strong tends to be favoured. |
| **Pace and aggression** | Aggression that scores matters, but constant forward movement without effect may not. |
| **Clean sweeps and dumps** | A clean dump that puts the opponent on the canvas can score. |

## Part XV — Rules and Rulesets (you need this before you compete)

Muay Thai has several rulesets. Always confirm before a fight.

| Ruleset | Strikes | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Traditional / Thai stadium rules** | Punches, kicks, knees, elbows, clinch, sweeps and throws. No strikes to the groin. | 5 rounds × 3 minutes, 2-minute breaks (Lumpinee and Rajadamnern style). Scoring favours kicks, knees, elbows and balance. |
| **International (IFMA-style amateur) rules** | Usually padded and with limits on elbows and clinch — **varies by class and event**. | Protective gear (headgear, shin and elbow pads) is common in amateur divisions. |
| **Muay Thai in western promotions** | Often 3 or 5 rounds; 10-point-must scoring. | Elbows usually allowed in professional bouts; some restrict clinch or throws. |
| **Muay Thai in MMA** | Standing portion of MMA | See the MMA plan. |

**Common pro details (verify per promotion):** 10-point-must scoring in many countries · 3-minute rounds · weights follow standard professional divisions · bare shins, gloves of varying ounce weight (generally heavier for heavier classes).

**Fouls (typical):** strikes to the groin · attacking the back of the head or spine · biting, eye gouging, spitting · attacking a downed fighter · holding the ropes · intentionally throwing the opponent out of the ring · hitting after the bell · spiking or slamming throws · head butts.

**Ending a bout:** KO · TKO (referee or doctor stoppage, often for cuts) · corner stoppage · decision · draw · disqualification · no contest.

## Part XVI — The Syllabus: What Goes Into a Class

### Tier 0 — Before anything (first day)
Wai kru etiquette · wraps, gloves, shin guards · mobility (hips, ankles, hamstrings) · how to hit a bag safely · the check rule · clinch safety (tap and release).

### Tier 1 — Foundations (weeks 1–6)
Stance and guard · footwork · jab, cross, hook · teep (both legs) · round kick (body, low) · return-to-guard · shadowboxing · pad work with the kru.

### Tier 2 — Close range (weeks 6–16)
Straight and curving knees · basic elbows (horizontal, upward, downward) · clinch posture, collar tie and pummelling · the check and the catch · light clinch rounds.

### Tier 3 — Combinations and counters (weeks 12–24)
Teep-to-kick patterns · switch kick · punch–kick combinations · sweeps and dumps · counter to every attack (Part XII) · technical sparring.

### Tier 4 — Strategy (weeks 20+)
Style selection (femur, tae, khao, sok, plam) · scoring awareness · pacing across 5 rounds · cutting the ring · southpaw vs orthodox.

### Tier 5 — Competition prep
Know the ruleset and its scoring · camp structure · weight management (safe) · fight-day routine · supervised hard sparring.

### Tier 6 — Off the mat
Shin, ankle and knee care · cut and hand care · neck training · concussion awareness · recovery.

## Part XVII — Class Templates

### 90-minute Thai-gym class
Wai kru and warm-up (jump rope, shadowboxing) (20) · technique block (20) · pad rounds with a partner or kru (25) · clinch (15) · conditioning and bag (5) · stretching (5).

### Sparring ladder
Teeps and hands → low kicks at 30% → all kicks at 50% → clinch with knees to the body → add elbows controlled (pads or padding) → hard sparring. Never skip a rung.

## Part XVIII — Gap Analysis: What The List Above Still Misses

### XVIII.a — Things missing from almost every syllabus

| Gap | Why it gets left out | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Posture and balance as a skill** | It's not a strike. | It decides the clinch and the scoring. |
| **The check as a first-class skill** | Offence is more fun. | A failed check is the commonest cause of injury and lost rounds. |
| **Scoring literacy** | Assumed. | Western fighters lose in Thai stadiums by training for the wrong judging criteria. |
| **Cut care** | Not glamorous. | Elbows cut; stopping the bleeding wins or loses fights. |
| **Ritual and etiquette** | Treated as optional. | Respecting the culture is part of the art. |
| **Training load and overtraining** | Camps in Thailand are punishing. | Injuries and burnout end careers. |
| **Weight management** | A secret. | Extreme cutting is a major cause of poor performance. |

### XVIII.b — Whole domains the tables above under-serve

| Domain | What's missing |
|---|---|
| **Self-defense** | Teeps, low kicks, elbows and the clinch work well; high kicks do not in street clothes. |
| **Muay Boran** | Historical forms and regional variants. |
| **MMA-specific Muay Thai** | Small gloves, cage walls, takedown defense, the clinch against a wrestler. See the MMA plan. |
| **Kids' and older-adult adaptations** | Non-contact versions, no head contact. |
| **Women's Muay Thai** | Parallel history, division structure and scoring differences. |

### XVIII.c — Techniques omitted on purpose

| Move | Status |
|---|---|
| **Strikes to the groin** | Illegal everywhere. |
| **Elbows and knees to a downed opponent** | Illegal. |
| **Flying and spinning showpiece strikes** | Occur rarely; high failure cost. |
| **Slams and spiking throws** | Illegal or dangerous. |

### XVIII.d — The honest final gap

The list is not the skill. A fighter who owns the teep, the round kick, the check, the straight knee, the collar tie and the horizontal elbow can beat someone with a hundred showy techniques. **Posture beats power. The check beats the kick. Balance wins rounds.**
