Every punch, footwork pattern and defense in boxing applies here; this file covers what kickboxing **adds** and **changes**. ## How to read the tables

### Difficulty (`Diff`)

| # | Level | What it means |
|:-:|---|---|
| **1** | Day one | Teachable in one class, usable that night. |
| **2** | Easy | A few weeks of drilling. Forgiving of mistakes. |
| **3** | Moderate | Needs timing, a setup, and a reaction to feed off. |
| **4** | Hard | Long setup chains, tight details, or specific attributes (flexibility, balance, explosiveness). |
| **5** | Expert | Specialist move. High failure cost. |

### How common (`Com`)

| # | Level | What it means |
|:-:|---|---|
| **5** | Everywhere | Seen every session. Non-optional. |
| **4** | Very common | Core of most people's games. |
| **3** | Common | Regular sighting. |
| **2** | Occasional | Situational or specialist. |
| **1** | Rare | Niche, elite-only, or ruleset-locked. |

**A note on honesty:** ratings are informed coaching judgment, not a peer-reviewed dataset. "Kickboxing" is an umbrella term for several different sports — a technique's commonness depends heavily on the ruleset (Part XII).

**Stance convention:** orthodox (left foot forward) unless stated. "Lead" = forward limb, "rear" = back limb.

> **Scope note — what "kickboxing" means here.** The atlas is written for **K-1 / GLORY-style rules** (punches + kicks + knees, no elbows, limited clinch), the dominant modern international form, and also covers **American full-contact**, **low-kick**, and **point-fighting** variants in Part XII. Eight-limb striking with elbows and a full clinch is **[Muay Thai](muay-thai.md)**.

## Part I — The Map

Kickboxing adds the legs to boxing, and the legs change the geometry. Kicks reach further than punches, so there are more ranges, and the same stance has to protect against both.

| Range | Distance | Primary tools | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kicking range (long)** | Only kicks reach | Teep, oblique, low kick, round kick, side kick | Where leg kicks accumulate damage and teeps manage distance |
| **Punching range (mid)** | Straight punches reach | Jab, cross, hook, kick-punch blends | The kickboxing exchange zone |
| **Close range (short)** | Hooks, uppercuts, knees reach | Short hooks, uppercuts, knees, elbows if legal | Where fights are won in K-1 rules |
| **Clinch** | Chest-to-chest | Knees, sweeps and throws (where legal) | In K-1 rules the clinch is brief; one knee, then break |

**The core rules of the sport:**
1. *The leg kick is the equaliser.* A well-placed low kick eventually takes away an opponent's movement.
2. *Never kick without a setup — and never leave the leg out.* The catch is the most common way to lose a kick exchange.
3. *Punches set up kicks; kicks set up punches.* The best combinations alternate hands and feet.
4. *Check, then counter.* Defending a kick without a plan to counter it is only half the defense.

## Part II — Stances, Guards and Weight Distribution

| Stance / guard | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Kickboxing stance (bladed, 60/40 forward)** | Feet shoulder-width, lead foot slightly angled in, weight about 50–60% on the rear leg, hands at the cheekbones, elbows in. | 1 | 5 |
| **Boxing-style stance (square-ish)** | More weight forward, front-heavy; strong punches but vulnerable to leg kicks. | 1 | 3 |
| **Muay Thai–style stance** | Upright, square, weight back, lead leg light for teeps and checks. | 2 | 3 |
| **Karate-style (blade) stance** | Narrow, side-on, light and bouncy — point-fighting stance. | 2 | 3 |
| **Southpaw** | Mirror image. A southpaw's lead foot is outside the orthodox opponent's lead foot — kicks and straight punches change lines. | 2 | 4 |
| **Switch stance** | Changing orthodox ↔ southpaw. Enables kicks from both sides and unusual angles. | 4 | 3 |
| **High guard** | Both gloves at the head; forearms vertical. Basic. | 1 | 5 |
| **Long guard / range guard** | Lead hand extended; deflect and range-find. | 2 | 4 |
| **Kick-check guard** | High guard with lead knee ready to lift and shin ready to block. | 2 | 5 |
| **Guard for open-hand scoring (point fighting)** | Hands low and mobile for speed. | 3 | 2 |

## Part III — Footwork

All the boxing footwork in **[boxing.md Part III](boxing.md)** applies. What kickboxing adds is **kick-ready** movement.

| Movement | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Step-and-drag** | Basic advance and retreat. | 1 | 5 |
| **Bounce / switch-step rhythm** | Light foot rhythm; the switch step is the basis of the lead-leg kick and the switch-kick. | 2 | 5 |
| **Switch step** | Hop the feet to change which leg is forward; loads the kick. | 3 | 4 |
| **Lateral circling** | Circle away from the opponent's rear leg and rear hand (the power side). | 2 | 5 |
| **Pivot** | Rotate on the lead foot to open an angle. Also the basis of the round kick's hip turn. | 3 | 5 |
| **Reset step** | Step back to reset your range after a kick. | 2 | 4 |
| **Cut-off step** | Step diagonally to cut the ring; the opponent has to run. | 3 | 4 |
| **Drop step** | Quick step-and-drop into a stance or level change. | 3 | 2 |
| **Crossover (cross step)** | Step the rear foot across the lead for a longer attack, then launch (spinning or side kick). | 4 | 3 |
| **Skip step / lead-leg skip** | Skip forward on the rear foot to cover distance for a kick. | 3 | 4 |
| **Stance reset (return to guard)** | After every kick: land balanced, hands up, feet at stance width. | 2 | 5 |

## Part IV — Hand Strikes

Everything in **[boxing.md Part IV](boxing.md)** applies: jab, cross, lead and rear hook, uppercuts, overhand, body shots. Kickboxing-specific adjustments:

| Punch | Kickboxing adjustment | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Jab** | Held slightly more upright; used to cover the teep and the kick entry. | 1 | 5 |
| **Cross** | Often thrown with a short step; pairs with the lead leg kick. | 1 | 5 |
| **Lead hook** | Shorter and tighter, since the guard is also protecting against kicks. | 2 | 5 |
| **Rear hook** | Used as an exit and to set the rear kick. | 3 | 4 |
| **Uppercut** | Fewer thrown; they leave the head high against knees. | 3 | 3 |
| **Overhand** | Effective against opponents who drop their guard to check kicks. | 3 | 4 |
| **Body shots (hook, straight)** | Used to drop the elbows before a head kick. | 3 | 4 |
| **Backfist (spinning, legal in some rule sets)** | A spinning hit with the back of the fist; legal in K-1 rules (barely used), full-contact (varies), and point-fighting. | 4 | 2 |
| **Hammer fist** | A downward strike; legal in some, banned in most. | 3 | 1 |
| **Superman punch** | Jumping straight punch (usually a step-up and cross). | 4 | 2 |

## Part V — Kicks

### V.a — Front-leg and push kicks (distance)

| Kick | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Teep (front push kick) — rear leg** | Knee up, foot pushes into the body (belly or chest); the hips drive. Interrupts forward movement. | 2 | 5 |
| **Lead-leg teep (jab kick)** | Quick push with the lead foot. Range-finding and rhythm-breaking. | 2 | 5 |
| **Oblique kick (lead leg, to the knee)** | A push kick with the toe turned out, delivered at the front of the opponent's lead knee. Destroys the leg's range and lead-leg weight-bearing. | 3 | 4 |
| **Teep to the face** | Push kick aimed at the chin. A heavy tool against a forward-leaning opponent. | 3 | 3 |
| **Side kick (lead or rear)** | A linear kick with the heel or the blade of the foot; strong distance keeper. | 3 | 4 |
| **Spinning back kick (to the body)** | Spin 180°, heel drives into the liver. A knockout kick. | 4 | 3 |
| **Switch teep** | A teep after a switch step; surprises. | 3 | 3 |
| **Stomp kick (to the foot)** | Stamp on the opponent's lead foot. Mostly MMA, banned or rarely used in kickboxing rules. | 2 | 1 |
| **Jumping front kick** | Jump into a push or ball-of-the-foot kick; seen in karate-influenced rules. | 4 | 1 |
| **Axe kick** | Raise the leg and bring the heel down onto the head or collarbone. | 4 | 2 |

### V.b — Round (roundhouse) kicks

| Kick | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Rear low kick (to the thigh)** | The signature kickboxing weapon: pivot, hip rotation, shin hits the outside of the thigh (or the calf). | 2 | 5 |
| **Lead low kick** | A faster, less powerful low kick off the lead leg; often a surprise. | 3 | 4 |
| **Inside low kick (to the inner thigh)** | Targets the inside of the lead leg; hits the groin in the wrong form, hence caution. | 3 | 3 |
| **Calf kick** | A low kick that targets the calf; recently very popular as a setup against a lead leg. | 2 | 5 |
| **Body kick (rear)** | Round kick to the ribs and liver. | 2 | 5 |
| **Body kick (lead)** | Fast, hits the opponent's near ribs. | 3 | 4 |
| **High kick (rear)** | Round kick to the head or neck. Requires flexibility, balance and timing. | 3 | 4 |
| **High kick (lead)** | A snapping head kick off the lead leg. | 4 | 3 |
| **Switch kick** | Switch step then kick with the (now) rear leg; surprise power from the lead side. | 3 | 5 |
| **Skip kick** | A skipping step into a round kick for a longer range. | 3 | 3 |
| **Question mark kick** | A kick that starts like a front kick and curves into a high round kick. | 4 | 2 |
| **Hook kick (from lead leg)** | A heel-first curving kick toward the back of the head. | 4 | 2 |
| **Jumping round kick** | Jumps to deliver the kick; seen in point-fighting and Taekwondo-style techniques. | 4 | 1 |
| **Spinning heel kick (spinning hook kick)** | Spin and sweep the heel at the head. Highlight-reel. | 5 | 1 |

### V.c — Specialised and flashy kicks

| Kick | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Crescent kick** | An arcing leg that sweeps inside-out or outside-in; used for deflection or surprise. | 4 | 1 |
| **Wheel kick (spinning)** | Spinning hook kick. | 5 | 1 |
| **Flying knee / flying kick** | Jumps for added reach. | 5 | 1 |
| **Tornado (cartwheel) kick** | A jump and turn to deliver a kick. Taekwondo/karate rules. | 5 | 1 |
| **Superman kick** | Jumping side or front kick; showpiece. | 5 | 1 |
| **Drop-and-kick (ducking kick)** | Dropping the level before a rear kick to a standing opponent's leg. | 4 | 1 |

**Kick targets:** head · neck · body (ribs, liver, solar plexus) · thigh (outer, inner) · calf · front of the knee (push or oblique kick to the knee is legal in some rules) · foot (stomp, rarely).

## Part VI — Knees and Elbows

Knees are legal in K-1 style rules, and **elbows are not**. (Elbows are legal in Muay Thai — see **[muay-thai.md](muay-thai.md)**.)

| Technique | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Straight knee (clinch)** | Pull the opponent into a driving knee to the belly or chest. | 2 | 4 |
| **Knee to the body (rear leg)** | A knee thrown while the hands hold the opponent's head. K-1 rules allow limited clinching (one knee). | 3 | 4 |
| **Skip knee** | A skipping step to cover distance, then a knee. | 3 | 4 |
| **Flying knee** | Jump into a knee at the head or chest. One of the highest-profile K-1 knockouts. | 4 | 3 |
| **Switch knee** | Switch step and drive the knee. | 3 | 3 |
| **Knee to the head (against a ducking opponent)** | Catch a bent-over opponent. | 3 | 3 |
| **Diagonal (curving) knee** | Curving into the ribs. | 3 | 3 |
| **Elbows** | Not legal under K-1 / GLORY rules; legal in Muay Thai, some full-contact rules, and MMA. | — | — |

## Part VII — Combinations

Notation: 1 jab · 2 cross · 3 lead hook · 4 rear hook · 5 lead uppercut · 6 rear uppercut · **LK** lead low kick · **RK** rear low kick · **BK** body kick (R/L) · **HK** head kick · **T** teep · **K** knee.

| Combination | What it does | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **1–2** | Basic punch pair. | 1 | 5 |
| **1–2–RK** | Punches draw the guard up; the rear low kick hits the thigh. The standard. | 2 | 5 |
| **1–2–3–RK** | Three punches then the kick. | 3 | 5 |
| **1–RK** | Jab then rear low kick. | 2 | 5 |
| **1–2–BK** | Body kick after a cross. | 2 | 5 |
| **1–2–HK** | Head kick after a cross; the opponent covers the hands. | 3 | 4 |
| **1–2–3–BK** | Lead hook then body kick; the hook turns the opponent into the kick. | 3 | 4 |
| **Teep–2** | Teep to stop the advance, cross over the top. | 2 | 5 |
| **LK–2** | Lead leg kick to cross. | 3 | 4 |
| **Switch kick–2** | Switch kick followed by a cross. | 3 | 4 |
| **Oblique–cross** | Oblique to the knee, then a cross as the opponent drops. | 3 | 4 |
| **Jab–calf kick** | Calf kick behind a jab; the jab hides the shin. | 2 | 5 |
| **1–2–BK–RK (low-high-low)** | The 'high-low' pattern: attack different levels. | 3 | 4 |
| **Teep–HK** | Teep and then a head kick, since the opponent's hands are going down to the teep. | 4 | 3 |
| **Body–body–head** | Body shots to drop the guard, then the head. | 3 | 4 |
| **2–3–RK** | Cross, hook, rear low kick; a power pattern. | 3 | 4 |
| **Cross–knee–hook** | Clinch combo allowed under K-1: single knee then separate. | 4 | 3 |
| **Spinning back kick off the jab** | Jab, step, spin back kick to the body. | 4 | 3 |
| **Lead hook–spinning back fist** | Counter pattern vs forward pressure. | 5 | 1 |
| **Double kick (same leg, different level)** | Low kick then high kick with the same leg. | 4 | 3 |
| **Kick-kick-punch** | LK, RK, then 1–2. | 3 | 3 |

**Rule of combinations:** change levels (low, mid, high), change sides (lead, rear), and end in a safe stance — not on the one leg.

## Part VIII — Defense

### VIII.a — Boxing defenses

All the **[boxing defenses](boxing.md)** apply to punches — slip, roll, parry, block, pull, pivot — with the head tucked and the guard ready to check.

### VIII.b — Defending kicks

| Defense | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Shin check (low kick)** | Lift the lead (or rear) knee and turn the shin out to meet the incoming kick shin-to-shin. The most important skill in kickboxing. | 2 | 5 |
| **Knee check** | Lift the knee to the chest to deflect the oblique or low kick. | 2 | 4 |
| **Step-back (distance)** | Retreat out of range; the kick whiffs. | 1 | 5 |
| **Lift and pivot** | Check and rotate to a counter angle. | 3 | 4 |
| **Elbow block (body kick)** | Drop the elbow and forearm tight against the ribs; absorb with the arm. | 1 | 5 |
| **Catch (body kick)** | Trap the kick under the arm or against the body; counter. | 3 | 5 |
| **Catch and sweep** | Catch and sweep the base leg to put the opponent down (where legal). | 3 | 4 |
| **Head-kick block (cover)** | Raise the glove and shoulder; absorb on the forearm or the glove. | 2 | 5 |
| **Duck under the head kick** | Duck and counter with the cross or an uppercut. | 3 | 3 |
| **Parry of the round kick** | A push on the kick's momentum, rare. | 4 | 1 |
| **Teep catch or push** | Jam or deflect the teep with the hands. | 2 | 4 |
| **Blocking the teep with the lead knee** | Lift the knee to shut off the push. | 2 | 4 |
| **Side step of the teep** | Pivot; the foot misses. | 3 | 4 |
| **Spinning-kick defense** | Cover and close the distance when the opponent spins. | 3 | 3 |

### VIII.c — Defending the clinch

| Defense | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Pre-clinch hand fighting** | Hands up, inside control, prevent the double collar tie. | 2 | 5 |
| **Frame and push-off** | Forearm in the chest to stop the clinch. | 2 | 4 |
| **Underhook escape** | Underhook and rotate out. | 3 | 4 |
| **Break and reset** | Step back when the referee splits. | 1 | 5 |

## Part IX — Counter-Striking

| Attack | Counter | Why it works | Diff | Com |
|---|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Jab** | Slip + cross; parry + cross | See boxing. | 3 | 5 |
| **Cross** | Slip + lead hook; pull + counter | See boxing. | 3 | 5 |
| **Rear low kick** | Check + cross (the leg is still in the air) | The kicker is balanced on the one leg. | 3 | 5 |
| **Rear low kick** | Catch + sweep or punch | The captured leg takes away their base. | 3 | 4 |
| **Rear low kick** | Lift the lead leg and counter-kick the standing leg (leg-kick counter) | Beat them to the same target. | 4 | 3 |
| **Body kick** | Catch + punch; step in with an elbow block | Catch and strike while they hop. | 3 | 5 |
| **Body kick** | Counter low kick to the base leg | Takes the base. | 4 | 3 |
| **High kick** | Duck + uppercut | The kicker is on one leg. | 4 | 3 |
| **Teep** | Parry + cross; sidestep + low kick | The teep leaves the lead leg busy. | 3 | 4 |
| **Oblique to the knee** | Pull the knee out of range; counter with a hook | The kicker has to retract. | 3 | 4 |
| **Advancing knee** | Step in with the shoulder and clinch | Kill the space. | 3 | 3 |
| **Spinning back kick** | Step back, counter with cross | The kicker's back is turned. | 4 | 3 |
| **Overhand** | Pull + counter | See boxing. | 3 | 3 |
| **Switch kick** | Check + cross | Their stance is unstable. | 3 | 4 |

**Counter-striking principle:** kicks are slow compared to punches; every kick gives a counter window while the kicker is on one leg.

## Part X — Clinch and Close-Range Work (K-1 / GLORY rules)

Clinching in K-1 / GLORY rules is *restricted*: typically one knee per clinch, then the referee separates. Take-down and throw rules vary.

| Technique | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Double collar tie (plum)** | Both hands behind the head; control and pull down into knees. | 2 | 4 |
| **Single collar tie** | One hand behind the neck; the other free for punches. | 2 | 4 |
| **Overhook / underhook** | Wrestling-style inside control. | 2 | 4 |
| **Knee from clinch** | Single knee to the body. | 2 | 4 |
| **Push-off** | Create space and kick. | 2 | 4 |
| **Sweeps (rules vary)** | A trip off the standing leg; legal in some full-contact rules. | 3 | 2 |
| **Pummeling** | Compete for inside position, no heavy hold. | 2 | 3 |

## Part XI — Styles (Archetypes)

| Style | What it is | Typical tools |
|---|---|---|
| **K-1 / Dutch style** | High punch volume, boxing-based, hard low kicks, high-tempo combinations. | 1–2 + LK, hooks, body kicks. |
| **Muay Thai–influenced (Thai-style in kickboxing)** | Teeps, body kicks, knees, posture-driven. | Teep, body kick, knee. |
| **Karate / kyokushin-influenced** | Distance control, linear angles, strong low kicks. | Oblique, side kick, straight punches. |
| **Taekwondo-influenced** | Speed, high kicks, point-fighting. | Switch kicks, head kicks, spin kicks. |
| **Point-fighter** | Light, fast, scoring clean touches. | Reverse punches, hook kicks. |
| **Counter-kicker** | Checks, catches and returns. | Checks, catches. |
| **Pressure fighter** | Moves forward behind punches. | Peekaboo, hooks, low kicks. |
| **Out-fighter** | Distance, teeps and obliques. | Teep, oblique, jab. |

## Part XII — Rules and Rulesets (you need this before you compete)

Kickboxing is a family of rulesets. Know which one before you train for it.

| Ruleset | Strikes | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Point-fighting (semi-contact / light-contact)** | Punches and kicks to the head and body at controlled contact; the referee stops the fight on a clean hit | Speed and scoring; not designed for knockouts |
| **Full contact (American)** | Punches and kicks above the belt; no low kicks | Hand and head kicks dominate |
| **Low-kick rules** | Full contact above the belt plus low kicks to the thighs | Bridges full contact and K-1 |
| **K-1 / GLORY rules** | Punches, kicks (low, body, head), knees to the body and head. No elbows. Limited clinch (one knee, then break) | Modern pro standard; 3 rounds of 3 min typical, longer for title fights |
| **Muay Thai rules** | See **[muay-thai.md](muay-thai.md)** | Elbows, clinch, sweeps |
| **Kickboxing in MMA** | Standing portion of MMA | See the MMA plan |

**Common K-1-style details (verify per promotion):** 3 × 3-minute rounds (title fights up to 5); 10-point-must scoring; knockdown counts (a fighter may be stopped after three knockdowns in a round or in the fight, depending on the promotion); no elbows; no head-butts; no strikes to the groin; no attacks on a downed opponent; gloves usually 10 oz.

**Scoring criteria (typical):** *damage* first (knockdowns, visible damage), then *effective aggression and volume of clean strikes*, then *ring generalship*. Leg kicks are scored by the damage they cause, not by volume alone.

**Weight classes:** vary by promotion. Most follow professional boxing/MMA divisions, from flyweight through heavyweight.

## Part XIII — The Syllabus: What Goes Into a Class

### Tier 0 — Before anything (first day)
Hand wraps, gloves, shin guards, mouthguard and cup · warm-up and mobility (hips and ankles!) · how to hit a bag safely · the check rule: shin to shin.

### Tier 1 — Foundations (weeks 1–4)
Stance and guard · step-and-drag footwork · jab, cross, hook · teep · rear low kick · return to guard after each strike · shadowboxing.

### Tier 2 — Kicks and checks (weeks 4–12)
Rear body kick · lead teep · calf kick · the shin check · punch-kick combinations 1–2–RK · pad work · light technical sparring (hands only, then legs).

### Tier 3 — Setups and defense (weeks 8–20)
Switch kick · oblique · head kick fundamentals · catching the kick · counter-striking · slips and parries from boxing · knees from the clinch.

### Tier 4 — Strategy (weeks 12+)
Level changes · feints · southpaw vs orthodox · range control · cutting the ring · rule-specific tactics.

### Tier 5 — Competition prep
Know your ruleset and its scoring · weight cutting safely · fight-day routine · supervised hard sparring.

### Tier 6 — Off the mat
Shin conditioning (progressive, never to the point of injury) · flexibility for head kicks · ankle and knee care · concussion awareness · recovery.

---

## Part XIV — Class Templates

### 60-minute class
Warm-up and jump rope (10) · shadowboxing with kicks (10) · technique block (15) · pad or bag rounds (15) · conditioning (5) · stretching (5).

### Sparring ladder
Hands-only → hands + teep → low kicks to the thigh at 30% → all kicks 50% → controlled sparring → hard sparring. Never skip a rung. Shin-on-shin contact in sparring should be light.

## Part XV — Gap Analysis: What The List Above Still Misses

### XV.a — Things missing from almost every syllabus

| Gap | Why it gets left out | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Kick-checking as a first-class skill** | Offence is more fun. | A failed check is the commonest cause of injury and lost fights. |
| **Catching and countering kicks** | Needs a partner who kicks properly. | The best counter-striking opportunity. |
| **Flexibility and hip mobility** | Treated as optional. | Head kicks and healthy hips depend on it. |
| **Shin and foot care** | Not glamorous. | Shin conditioning abuse leads to stress fractures. |
| **Rules literacy** | Assumed. | Gym habits (elbows, clinching) get fighters disqualified under K-1 rules. |
| **Southpaw preparation** | Most gyms are orthodox. | A southpaw changes every kick and every angle. |
| **Weight management** | Treated as a secret. | Extreme cutting is a major cause of poor performance. |

### XV.b — Whole domains the tables above under-serve

| Domain | What's missing |
|---|---|
| **Self-defense** | Kicks above the waist are risky in street clothes; teeps, low kicks and punches matter more. |
| **MMA-specific striking** | Cage walls, takedown defense, small gloves. See the MMA plan. |
| **Kids' and older-adult adaptations** | Lower-impact training, no head contact. |

### XV.c — Techniques omitted on purpose

| Move | Status |
|---|---|
| **Stomps on the foot** | Banned in most kickboxing rules; MMA only. |
| **Strikes to the back of the head / spine** | Illegal. |
| **Kicks to the groin** | Illegal in all rules. |
| **Knee to the head of a grounded opponent** | Banned in kickboxing; MMA-ruleset dependent. |
| **Flying and spinning showpiece kicks** | Occur rarely; high failure cost. |

### XV.d — The honest final gap

The list is not the skill. A fighter who owns the jab, the cross, the rear low kick, the teep, the shin check and the catch can beat someone with fifty flashy techniques. **Depth beats breadth. The check beats the kick. The leg kick wins fights slowly.**
