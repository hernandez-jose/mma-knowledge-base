# Escapes

Seven positional escape systems, one page each. Every page covers why the
position works, the survival rules, each escape from it, **how the escape
changes against much bigger or much smaller opponents**, failure modes, drills
and safety.

## Why these come first

[`jujitsu.md`](../../jujitsu.md) makes the argument plainly: escapes are the
highest-return skill in jiu-jitsu for the first two years. You cannot attack
from a position you can't survive, and the confidence to play an offensive game
comes entirely from knowing you can get out when it fails.

Escapes are also where body size matters most. Escaping a 60 kg opponent and a
110 kg opponent are genuinely different problems — different leverage, different
timing, different escape entirely in some cases. Most instruction pretends they
are the same. Section 4 of every page here does not.

## The universal rules

These apply in every position on this list, and every page restates them in
context:

1. **Frames before force.** Bone structures — forearms, shins — hold space without burning energy. Muscle tires; skeleton doesn't. This is how smaller people survive bigger people.
2. **Elbows connected to knees.** The moment your elbow leaves your ribs, space opens for them, not you.
3. **Breathe.** Panic is mostly oxygen debt. Nasal, rhythmic, continuous — especially under a heavy top player.
4. **Hips move first.** Shrimping and bridging are the engine of every escape here.
5. **Escape early.** Every position gets harder to leave the longer you're in it. The best moment to escape is before they've settled.
6. **Tap early on submissions.** Escaping is a skill; escaping a fully locked submission is a gamble.
