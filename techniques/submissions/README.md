# Submissions

22 finishing techniques, one page each. Every page covers the mechanism, the
standard application, **how to adjust it for opponents of different body sizes**,
failure modes, chains, defense, drills and safety.

Ratings match [`jujitsu.md`](../../jujitsu.md): **Diff** = how hard to pull off
(1 = day one, 5 = expert). **Com** = how often you'll see it (1 = rare,
5 = everywhere).

## Leg locks

> **Read the safety section on every one of these pages before drilling them.**
> Apply slowly — there is little warning before a knee ligament tears — and tap
> early. Legality varies sharply by belt and ruleset; the straight ankle lock is
> the only leg lock legal at every IBJJF belt.

| Submission | From | Diff | Com | IBJJF legal |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|---|
| [Straight ankle lock](straight-ankle-lock.md) | Outside ashi, SLX | 2 | 4 | all belts |

## If you only learn five

The data in [`jujitsu.md`](../../jujitsu.md) is blunt about this: a small number
of submissions account for most finishes in competition. Start here.

1. **[Rear naked choke](rear-naked-choke.md)** — highest-percentage finish in grappling
2. **[Armbar](armbar.md)** — the most common joint lock, available from five positions
3. **[Triangle choke](triangle-choke.md)** — top-three finish everywhere
4. **[Guillotine](guillotine.md)** — the one you'll catch off failed takedowns
5. **[Arm triangle](arm-triangle.md)** — the most reliable finish from mount and side control
