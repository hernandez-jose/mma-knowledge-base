## How to read the tables

Identical to the Jiu-Jitsu atlas, so ratings compare across arts.

### Difficulty (`Diff`)

| # | Level | What it means |
|:-:|---|---|
| **1** | Day one | A total beginner can be taught this in one class and use it that night. |
| **2** | Easy | A few weeks of drilling. Forgiving of mistakes. |
| **3** | Moderate | Needs timing, a setup, and a reaction to feed off. |
| **4** | Hard | Long setup chains, tight details, or specific attributes (speed, reflexes, ring IQ). |
| **5** | Expert | Specialist move. High failure cost — miss it and you eat a counter. |

### How common (`Com`)

| # | Level | What it means |
|:-:|---|---|
| **5** | Everywhere | Seen or felt every session. Non-optional knowledge. |
| **4** | Very common | Core of most people's games. |
| **3** | Common | Regular sighting. |
| **2** | Occasional | Situational or specialist. |
| **1** | Rare | Niche, elite-only, or style-specific. Worth knowing exists. |

**A note on honesty:** `Com` and `Diff` are informed coaching judgment, not a peer-reviewed dataset. Boxing has no public technique-frequency database comparable to BJJ's; where a number looks precise, it's a rule of thumb. Your mileage varies with body type, stance and ruleset.

**Stance convention:** every description assumes **orthodox** (left foot and left hand forward). Southpaw is a mirror — see Part II. "Lead" = forward hand/foot, "rear" = back hand/foot.

**Numbering system (gym standard):** 1 = jab · 2 = cross · 3 = lead hook · 4 = rear hook · 5 = lead uppercut · 6 = rear uppercut. Body shots are called "to the body" (e.g. "3 to the body"). Some gyms swap 5/6; confirm with your coach.

## Part I — The Map

Boxing is a fight over **distance, angle and timing**. Every exchange happens in one of four ranges, and each range has its own tools.

| Range | Distance | Primary tools | Danger |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Long (outside)** | Out of reach of both fighters; a step covers it | Jab, feints, footwork, ring cutting | Low — pure setup range |
| **Mid (punching)** | Straight punches land at full extension | Jab, cross, lead hook, counters | Highest — where knockouts happen |
| **Short (inside)** | Elbows nearly touching, hooks and uppercuts reach | Hooks, uppercuts, body shots, slips | High — heavy exchanges, head clashes |
| **Clinch** | Chest-to-chest, arms tied | Holding, pushing, short shots when allowed | Low damage — a reset and a rest |

**The core rules of the sport:**
1. *Hit and don't get hit.* Defense is half the sport and the half that lasts into your forties.
2. *Don't punch from where you stand — punch from where you've moved to.* Angles beat strength.
3. *Win the jab, win the range.* The jab decides who is dictating.
4. *Hands return the way they left.* Every punch ends in guard.

## Part II — Stances and Guards

### II.a — Stances

| Stance | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Orthodox** | Left foot and left hand forward, right hand is the power hand. Used by most right-handers. | 1 | 5 |
| **Southpaw** | Right foot and right hand forward. About 10% of the population; awkward to fight if you rarely face it. | 1 | 4 |
| **Switch stance** | Changing orthodox to southpaw mid-fight on purpose; hard to do without losing power or balance. | 4 | 2 |
| **Square / open stance** | Feet roughly parallel, chest facing the opponent. Obsolete in boxing (exposes the whole body); a common amateur habit to train out. | 1 | 3 |
| **Bladed / side-on stance** | Narrow chest profile, rear hand protects chin from the shoulder. Defensive, used by long-range boxers and fighters with a big jab. | 2 | 3 |
| **Crouched / peekaboo stance** | Low, forward-leaning, hands high on cheeks, head moving. Pressure fighters; Mike Tyson era. | 3 | 3 |
| **Philly shell** | Lead arm across the belly, rear hand at the chin, lead shoulder high. A counterpuncher's stance. | 4 | 2 |
| **Upright (Cuban) stance** | Tall, light on the feet, extremely mobile. Amateur-style out-boxing. | 3 | 3 |

**Foot position:** shoulder-width, rear heel lifted, weight roughly 50/50 to 60/40 depending on style, knees soft. The lead-foot toe points at the opponent; rear foot at roughly 45°.

### II.b — Guards (hand positions)

| Guard | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **High guard (basic)** | Both fists at the cheekbones, elbows in, chin tucked. The default. | 1 | 5 |
| **Long guard** | Lead arm extended and relaxed as a range-finder, rear hand at the chin. Out-boxers. | 2 | 4 |
| **Peekaboo (high cover)** | Gloves glued to the face, elbows tight. Pairs with a bob-and-weave. | 3 | 3 |
| **Philly shell / shoulder roll guard** | See stances. Lead shoulder rolls off punches; rear hand parries. | 4 | 2 |
| **Cross-arm guard** | Both forearms crossed in front of the chest and chin. Dated, used to soak body shots. | 3 | 1 |
| **Hands-low / Ali-Roy Jones guard** | Hands at the belt or hips, head reading distance. Needs elite reflexes and rhythm. Dangerous for anyone else. | 5 | 1 |

## Part III — Footwork

Boxing is a footwork art that happens to have hands. Footwork brings you to the range and the angle; hands are the last step.

### III.a — Basic movement

| Movement | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Step-and-drag (advance)** | Lead foot steps first, rear foot follows immediately. Stance width stays constant. | 1 | 5 |
| **Retreat (back step)** | Rear foot goes first, lead foot follows. | 1 | 5 |
| **Lateral step (side step)** | Move off the centreline, left or right, same step-and-drag rule. | 1 | 5 |
| **Shuffle** | Quick short advance, push off the rear foot; the basis of every jab. | 2 | 5 |
| **Bounce / ball-of-the-foot rhythm** | Small continual bounces to stay loaded and break the opponent's timing. | 1 | 4 |
| **Switch step** | Switching the feet by hopping lead to rear; changes stance or surprises with a power shot. | 3 | 3 |
| **Skip / hop-in** | A tiny hop to cover distance while keeping the guard. | 3 | 3 |
| **Slide step** | A long gliding advance for a lunging jab or straight right. | 3 | 3 |
| **Crossover step** | A deliberate cross step to cover large distance or flee the ropes. Risky: you are momentarily unbalanced. | 3 | 2 |
| **Lateral shuffle (circling)** | Circle away from the power hand: for orthodox, move left, away from the opponent's right. | 2 | 5 |
| **Circle toward the power hand** | The opposite; for orthodox, step right, towards the opponent's right hand. Only used with a counter in mind. | 3 | 3 |

### III.b — Angles and positioning

| Concept | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Pivot (lead foot)** | Rotate on the ball of the lead foot to turn your body around the opponent and open an angle. The core angle tool. | 3 | 5 |
| **Pivot (rear foot)** | Rotate on the rear foot to escape pressure and counter. | 3 | 4 |
| **Step-out angle** | Step diagonally out of the line of fire and hit from the side. | 3 | 4 |
| **Outside foot position** | Placing your lead foot outside the opponent's lead foot so your lead hand and rear hand both have a path and theirs do not. | 3 | 5 |
| **Inside foot position** | Lead foot inside their lead foot — you are on their centreline, and so are they. | 3 | 4 |
| **Cut-off (ring generalship)** | Stepping diagonally to shorten the opponent's retreat rather than chasing in a line. | 3 | 5 |
| **Rope escape / pivot off the ropes** | Turn the opponent into the ropes via a pivot and slip out. | 3 | 4 |
| **Corner escape** | Same, in a corner. Use the rear foot pivot and a shoulder roll. | 4 | 3 |
| **Backing up in a straight line** | The most common amateur error: retreating in a straight line gives free shots. Fix: always leave at an angle. | — | 5 |

## Part IV — Punches

### IV.a — The six fundamentals

| Punch | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Jab (1)** | Straight lead-hand punch; shoulder rotates, rear hand stays at the chin, the fist turns over at impact. Range-finder, defender, setup, counter. | 1 | 5 |
| **Cross (2)** | Straight rear-hand punch; pushes off the rear foot, hip and shoulder rotation, chin tucked behind the lead shoulder. The primary power straight. | 1 | 5 |
| **Lead hook (3)** | Short horizontal arc with the lead hand, elbow bent about 90°, thrown with the whole body turning on the lead foot. | 2 | 5 |
| **Rear hook (4)** | The same with the rear hand; harder and slower, easy to telegraph. | 3 | 4 |
| **Lead uppercut (5)** | Short upward drive with the lead hand from a slight knee dip. Used inside. | 3 | 4 |
| **Rear uppercut (6)** | Upward drive with the rear hand; hits the chin or solar plexus at close range. | 3 | 4 |

### IV.b — Jab variations

| Variation | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Standard jab** | Described above. | 1 | 5 |
| **Double / triple jab** | Repeated jabs, often changing speed or height. Controls range and hides the third. | 2 | 5 |
| **Power jab** | A jab thrown with a step and body weight. Can stop a charge. | 2 | 4 |
| **Flick / snap jab** | A fast loose jab from the long guard to annoy and measure distance. | 2 | 4 |
| **Pawing jab** | Open, low-commitment jab to set a grip on the opponent's guard or block vision. | 1 | 3 |
| **Stiff jab (pushing jab)** | Held through impact to keep the opponent at range. | 2 | 4 |
| **Jab to the body** | Dip the knees and jab to the solar plexus. Changes level. | 2 | 4 |
| **Step-in jab (lunging)** | Jab thrown with a long step; the main distance closer. | 3 | 4 |
| **Backing-away jab** | Jab thrown while retreating; the sign of a trained boxer. | 4 | 3 |
| **Jab off the pivot** | Pivot to the outside, jab from the new angle. | 3 | 3 |
| **Jab as a defensive tool** | Jab into an incoming punch to stop it or spoil its timing. | 3 | 4 |
| **Southpaw lead-hand jab** | The same, but from the opposite stance; lead with the right hand. | 2 | 4 |

### IV.c — Cross and straight-right variations

| Variation | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Standard cross** | See above. | 1 | 5 |
| **Step-in cross** | The cross with a lead-foot slide to close range. | 3 | 4 |
| **Cross to the body** | Bent-knee level change, right hand into the liver-side. | 3 | 4 |
| **Counter cross (over the jab)** | Slip the jab to the outside; the cross travels over the jab. Arguably the most-used counter in boxing. | 3 | 5 |
| **Counter cross (inside the jab)** | Cross inside the lead arm after a parry. | 3 | 4 |
| **Lunging straight right** | A long-range committed cross with a step. Big reward, big risk. | 4 | 3 |
| **Southpaw straight left (left-straight)** | Lead-leg southpaw's lethal punch. Orthodox fighters struggle against it. | 2 | 4 |

### IV.d — Hook variations

| Variation | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Lead hook to the head** | The core 3. | 2 | 5 |
| **Lead hook to the body (liver shot)** | Hook thrown low to the opponent's right ribs; a fight-ender. | 3 | 5 |
| **Rear hook to the body** | Hook to the opponent's lead-side (left ribs). Harder to land; hurts. | 4 | 3 |
| **Check hook** | Lead hook thrown while pivoting out of the way of a charging opponent; both a counter and a defense. | 4 | 3 |
| **Left hook off the jab** | Jab to blind, then step in with the hook. | 3 | 5 |
| **Left hook over the right** | A disguised shot used as the second half of a counter. | 4 | 3 |
| **Rear (right) hook** | The 4. | 3 | 4 |
| **Short hook (in-close)** | Compact hook with a bent elbow and nearly no windup. | 3 | 4 |
| **Long / wide hook (looping)** | Wide, powerful, telegraphed. A poor choice against a good boxer. | 2 | 3 |
| **Drag hook** | Pull the opponent's head or arm and hook around the guard. | 4 | 2 |
| **Hook around the guard** | A hook arcing around the glove to the temple. | 3 | 4 |
| **Hook to the temple vs. hook to the jaw** | Different trajectories; temple hooks arc downward, jaw hooks horizontal. | 3 | 4 |
| **Ripping hook** | Hook with maximal rotation, pulling the hand across the body. | 4 | 2 |

### IV.e — Uppercut variations

| Variation | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Lead uppercut (5)** | Short, from a lowered lead shoulder. | 3 | 4 |
| **Rear uppercut (6)** | Hip drives the fist upward; effective when the opponent bends forward. | 3 | 4 |
| **Body uppercut** | Dip and drive the fist upward into the solar plexus. | 3 | 3 |
| **Uppercut counter (against a body-bent opponent)** | Catch an opponent who ducks. | 4 | 4 |
| **Shovel hook** | Compact diagonal blend of hook and uppercut, thrown to the body or chin at close range. | 4 | 3 |
| **Rising uppercut off the roll** | After rolling under a hook, the rear uppercut as you come up. | 4 | 3 |
| **Uppercut to the head through the guard** | Lifting the glove to open the chin. | 4 | 3 |

### IV.f — Looping, overhead and specialty punches

| Punch | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Overhand right** | Looping rear-hand punch over the opponent's lead hand; thrown from outside the jab, favoured by pressure and slow-footed power punchers. | 3 | 4 |
| **Southpaw overhand left** | Mirror in the other stance. | 3 | 3 |
| **Bolo punch** | A wide looping uppercut that starts with a circular wind-up and finishes upward; mostly a showpiece, a distraction when used well. | 5 | 1 |
| **Corkscrew punch** | A twisting overhand that rotates on impact; intended to cut the face. | 4 | 1 |
| **Check-and-pivot hook** | See check hook. | 4 | 3 |
| **Rabbit punch** | Strike to the back of the head or neck. Illegal — listed so you know to avoid it. | — | — |
| **Chopping right** | A short overhand-style strike thrown downward, used against a lunging or ducking opponent. | 4 | 2 |
| **Cut shot (cutter)** | A lead uppercut or hook thrown to a precise target (the eyebrow) to open a cut. Often a deliberate spoiler. | 4 | 2 |
| **Jolt** | A short, sharp lead-hand punch with the weight on the front foot. A stinging, not a knockout, punch. | 2 | 3 |
| **Flicker jab** | Jab thrown from a low hand position, loose and snapped back; deceptive angle and hard to read. | 3 | 2 |
| **Gazelle punch** | A lunging punch thrown while springing forward off the rear foot to cover extra distance. Flashy and balance-costly. | 5 | 1 |
| **Dempsey roll** | A continuous side-to-side weave in a figure-eight pattern, loading hooks as you come up; historical, still taught. | 4 | 1 |
| **Superman punch** | A jump into a straight punch. Not recommended; broken balance. | 4 | 1 |
| **Sucker punch / blind-side** | A punch from outside the opponent's field of view. Legal in the ring, ethically stupid outside. | 1 | 2 |

### IV.g — Body punching

| Target | Punch | Why | Diff | Com |
|---|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Liver** (right rib cage, opponent's right side) | Lead hook to the body | A hard shot paralyses the breath. A fight-ender. | 3 | 5 |
| **Solar plexus** (centre, under the sternum) | Straight to the body | Winds the opponent. | 2 | 4 |
| **Spleen / left floating ribs** | Rear hook to the body | Heavy ribs shot; breaks ribs and the will. | 4 | 3 |
| **Stomach** | Uppercuts and straight punches | Cumulative. Lowers the guard. | 2 | 4 |
| **Body-to-head switch** | Double-up: hit the body to drop the hands, then hit the head | The staple of every pressure fighter. | 3 | 5 |

## Part V — Combinations

Combinations are described by number (see the gym numbering system above). Always end with guard up or an exit.

| Combination | Numbers | What it does | Diff | Com |
|---|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Jab–cross** | 1–2 | The basic. Jab opens the guard, cross hits. | 1 | 5 |
| **Double jab–cross** | 1–1–2 | The second jab hides the cross. | 2 | 5 |
| **Jab–cross–hook** | 1–2–3 | The classic three-punch; the hook hits as the opponent backs up from the cross. | 2 | 5 |
| **Jab–cross–hook–cross** | 1–2–3–2 | Finishing cross after a hook; the opponent is shaken sideways. | 3 | 4 |
| **Jab–hook** | 1–3 | A short combination; the jab blinds and the hook lands. | 2 | 4 |
| **Jab–body hook–head hook** | 1–3b–3 | Body-to-head. | 3 | 4 |
| **Jab–cross–slip–cross** | 1–2–slip–2 | Defensive combo; slip a counter, cross again. | 3 | 4 |
| **Jab–cross–lead uppercut–cross** | 1–2–5–2 | The uppercut raises the chin; the cross lands. | 3 | 3 |
| **Hook–cross–hook** | 3–2–3 | A power triple: the hook turns the opponent, the cross lands, the hook finishes. | 3 | 4 |
| **Cross–hook–cross** | 2–3–2 | A mid-range power sequence. | 3 | 4 |
| **Lead hook–rear uppercut–lead hook** | 3–6–3 | Close-range triple. | 4 | 3 |
| **Uppercut–hook** | 5–3 | Common inside pair. | 3 | 4 |
| **Triple hook** | 3–4–3 | Continuous hooks inside. | 4 | 3 |
| **Body-body-head** | 2b–3b–3 | Low, low, high. | 3 | 4 |
| **Jab–cross–hook–uppercut–hook** | 1–2–3–6–3 | The 'long' combination. | 4 | 3 |
| **Jab to body–jab to head** | 1b–1 | Changing levels; the second jab freezes the guard. | 2 | 4 |
| **Pivot combination** | 1–2–pivot–3 | Combination ending in an angle exit. | 4 | 4 |
| **Pull counter** | pull–2 | Lean back from a jab; the cross counters. | 4 | 4 |
| **Check hook–cross** | check 3–2 | Counter against a charge. | 4 | 3 |
| **Southpaw 1–2 (jab–left straight)** | 1–2 | Southpaw's basic. | 2 | 5 |
| **Southpaw lead-hand slip–2** | slip–2 | Slip and straight left. | 3 | 4 |
| **Flurry (volley)** | any | Ten or more punches thrown while the opponent is hurt. Should end on a clean shot. | 3 | 4 |

**Rule of combinations:** change level, change angle, change speed. Three punches in a straight line at the same height are easy to defend.

## Part VI — Defense

Boxing defense falls into five groups: *don't be there* (distance), *take it on the gloves* (blocks and covers), *deflect it* (parries), *let it pass* (slips, rolls, pulls), and *move out* (footwork). A good boxer uses all five.

### VI.a — Distance and footwork defense

| Defense | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Out-of-range retreat** | Step back out of reach so the punch falls short. | 1 | 5 |
| **Pull / lean back** | Lean the torso back to let the punch pass; keep the feet planted. | 2 | 4 |
| **Side step / lateral step** | Step off the line of attack. | 2 | 5 |
| **Pivot out** | Turn around the opponent's lead foot. | 3 | 5 |
| **Circling** | Move continuously off the power hand. | 2 | 5 |
| **Clinch to smother** | Tie up when under pressure. | 1 | 5 |

### VI.b — Blocks and covers

| Defense | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **High block / cover-up** | Both gloves at the face; absorb the punch on the gloves and forearms. | 1 | 5 |
| **Elbow block (body)** | Drop the elbows to protect the ribs and liver. | 1 | 5 |
| **Forearm block** | The forearm takes the blow. | 1 | 4 |
| **Cross-arm cover** | Both forearms crossed in front. | 2 | 2 |
| **Catch (glove catch)** | Catch the punch in the glove (especially the jab). | 2 | 4 |
| **Shell (shoulder shell)** | Lead shoulder rotates up to protect the chin; see Philly shell. | 3 | 3 |
| **Peekaboo cover** | Gloves tight on cheekbones, elbows close, a head-moving defense. | 3 | 3 |
| **Rear-hand cover on the lead side** | The rear glove protects the lead jaw against a lead hook. | 1 | 5 |

### VI.c — Parries

| Parry | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Inside (rear-hand) parry vs jab** | Push the incoming jab down or aside with the rear glove (palm out). | 2 | 5 |
| **Outside (lead-hand) parry vs jab** | Parry the jab with the lead hand to the outside. | 2 | 4 |
| **Cross parry (lead hand)** | Redirect the cross with the lead glove to the outside. | 3 | 4 |
| **Downward parry** | Parry downward to keep the guard high and set up the counter jab. | 2 | 4 |
| **Catch-and-pitch** | Catch a punch, push it aside and return. | 3 | 3 |
| **Hook parry (check)** | Block the elbow or the glove of a hook with the lead hand. | 3 | 3 |
| **Uppercut parry (downward pat)** | Pat the uppercut down with the open palm. | 3 | 3 |
| **Body-shot elbow block** | Elbow comes down to sweep a body hook aside. | 2 | 4 |

### VI.d — Slips, rolls and pulls

| Defense | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Slip to the outside (vs jab)** | Rotate the shoulder and head just past the punch; leaves your rear hand free. | 3 | 5 |
| **Slip to the inside (vs jab)** | Head moves to the inside — counter possibilities open. | 4 | 3 |
| **Slip (vs cross)** | Move the head to the outside, over the lead shoulder. | 3 | 5 |
| **Roll (bob and weave)** | Dip under a hook, travel in a U-shape, come up outside. | 3 | 4 |
| **Duck** | Drop the level under a punch; keep the eyes up. | 2 | 4 |
| **Weave** | Lateral version of the roll, forward and sideways. | 3 | 4 |
| **Shoulder roll (lead)** | The lead shoulder rotates up; the punch glances off. Works best against crosses. | 4 | 3 |
| **Pull counter** | Pull back, then fire the cross. | 4 | 4 |
| **Lean-back slip** | Lean back and sideways, used mostly against the jab. | 3 | 3 |
| **Slip-and-rip** | Slip the jab and attack the body with the lead hook. | 4 | 4 |
| **Slip-and-slide (outside)** | Slip and step to the outside. | 3 | 4 |

### VI.e — Clinch and holding as defense

| Defense | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Clinch tie-up (overhook/underhook)** | Smother punches and rest. | 1 | 5 |
| **Pinning the glove** | Hold the opponent's glove against his chest to stop a combination. | 2 | 3 |
| **Pushing off** | Use the forearm in the chest to create distance; keep the elbow tight. | 2 | 4 |
| **Spin out** | Turn the opponent off the ropes with a pivot. | 3 | 4 |

## Part VII — Counter-Punching

A counter is a punch thrown in the moment an opponent's attack is committed. The three timing windows: *before* (interception), *during* (counter-in-the-gap) and *after* (return).

### VII.a — Counters to the jab

| Counter | Mechanics | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Slip + cross** | Slip outside, rotate and throw the cross over the jab. | 3 | 5 |
| **Parry + cross** | Parry with the rear hand and fire the cross. | 2 | 5 |
| **Pull + cross** | Pull back, then throw the cross. | 4 | 4 |
| **Jab to the jab** | Beat the jab with a faster, shorter jab. | 3 | 4 |
| **Slip + body hook** | Slip outside, then hook the liver. | 4 | 4 |
| **Duck + hook** | Duck under the jab and hook. | 3 | 3 |
| **Shoulder roll + rear hand** | Shoulder takes the jab; a counter cross. | 4 | 3 |
| **Catch + counter cross** | Catch the jab in the glove, throw the cross. | 3 | 4 |

### VII.b — Counters to the cross

| Counter | Mechanics | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Slip + lead hook** | Slip outside and hook as the opponent is off balance. | 3 | 5 |
| **Slip inside + uppercut** | Inside slip, rear uppercut. | 4 | 3 |
| **Pull + counter cross** | Lean back, return the cross. | 4 | 4 |
| **Parry + lead hook** | Parry the cross with the lead hand and hook. | 3 | 4 |
| **Shoulder roll + hook** | Roll the cross off the lead shoulder and hit the exposed jaw. | 4 | 3 |
| **Duck + body hook** | Duck under, rip the body. | 3 | 3 |
| **Step-back + jab** | A jab as the cross falls short. | 3 | 4 |

### VII.c — Counters to the hook

| Counter | Mechanics | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Roll under + hook** | Roll under the hook and counter hook. | 4 | 4 |
| **Duck + cross** | Duck under the hook, fire the cross. | 3 | 4 |
| **Pull + jab** | Lean back; the jab catches the opponent coming forward. | 3 | 3 |
| **Block + cross** | Cover-up the hook and answer with the cross. | 2 | 4 |
| **Step-in cross over the hook** | Step inside the arc and throw the straight. | 4 | 3 |
| **Check hook** | Hook with a pivot to meet a rushing opponent. | 4 | 3 |
| **Counter hook to hook** | Meet the hook with a faster, shorter one. | 4 | 3 |

### VII.d — Counters to the uppercut and the body shot

| Counter | Mechanics | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Pull + jab** | Lean back from the uppercut. | 3 | 3 |
| **Parry + hook** | Pat down, hook. | 3 | 3 |
| **Elbow block + hook** | Elbow takes the body shot, hook upstairs. | 3 | 3 |
| **Step back + straight punch** | The body hook falls short; the straight lands. | 3 | 4 |
| **Underhook + uppercut** | Clinch and chop the chin. | 3 | 2 |
| **Lean-on-the-opponent (smother)** | Smother the body shot. | 2 | 4 |

## Part VIII — Offense Systems and Setups

| System | What it is | Diff | Com |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|
| **Feints (jab, shoulder, foot, eye)** | Fake an action to draw a reaction. 90% of good boxing. | 3 | 5 |
| **Range-finder jab** | Light jabs to read distance and reaction. | 2 | 5 |
| **Pressure fighting** | Walk the opponent down, cut the ring, body work. | 3 | 4 |
| **Counter-punching** | Let the opponent lead and punish it. | 4 | 4 |
| **Out-boxing (boxer)** | Long range, jab and movement. | 4 | 4 |
| **Swarming (in-fighting)** | Constant volume from short range. | 3 | 3 |
| **Slugging** | Power exchanges, little defense. High-risk. | 2 | 3 |
| **Body-first strategy** | Invest in the body early to open the head later. | 3 | 4 |
| **Setting traps** | Show a habit, then punish the habitual response. | 4 | 3 |
| **Broken-rhythm attack** | Change the tempo and timing of combinations. | 4 | 4 |
| **Southpaw vs orthodox tactics** | Lead-foot positioning, lead-hand control. | 3 | 4 |
| **Switch-hitting** | Alternating stances. | 4 | 2 |
| **Cutting the ring** | Angled steps rather than straight chase. | 3 | 5 |
| **Dictating the pace** | Controlling the rhythm of the exchange. | 3 | 4 |
| **Fighting off the ropes (rope-a-dope)** | Lean on the ropes and absorb. Dangerous; Ali made it famous in 1974. | 5 | 1 |

## Part XII — Rules and Rulesets (you need this before you compete)

### XII.a — The main rulesets

| Feature | Amateur (Olympic / World Boxing style) | Professional |
|---|---|---|
| **Rounds** | Typically 3 rounds of 3 minutes in elite men's and women's bouts | 4–12 rounds, 3 minutes each for men; 2-minute rounds are common in women's pro bouts, with 3-minute rounds in some title fights |
| **Scoring** | Usually the 10-point-must system, scored round by round | 10-point-must system |
| **Headgear** | Not used in elite adult male amateur boxing; commonly used in youth and club boxing | Not used |
| **Gloves** | Typically 10 oz in elite amateur bouts | Often 8 oz up to the welterweight class and 10 oz above |
| **Protection** | Mouthguard, cup, wraps | Same, plus cut-man corner care |
| **Referee** | Can stop the fight on a standing count | Can stop; doctor can stop on cut or concussion risk |

### XII.b — Weight classes (professional, rounded)

Minimumweight 105 lb · Light flyweight 108 · Flyweight 112 · Super flyweight 115 · Bantamweight 118 · Super bantamweight 122 · Featherweight 126 · Super featherweight 130 · Lightweight 135 · Super lightweight 140 · Welterweight 147 · Super welterweight 154 · Middleweight 160 · Super middleweight 168 · Light heavyweight 175 · Cruiserweight 200 (some sanctioning bodies) · Heavyweight above 200 (some bodies add a bridgerweight division). Amateur classes differ per federation — check the current one.

### XII.c — Scoring

Under the **10-point-must system**, the round's winner gets 10 points and the loser 9 or fewer. Knockdowns usually cost a further point. A drawn round is 10–10. Judges weigh *clean punching, effective aggression, ring generalship and defense* (the criteria vary by commission). The most common error: scoring volume over clean hitting.

### XII.d — Fouls (typical across commissions)

Low blows (below the belt) · hitting after the bell · holding and hitting · hitting the back of the head (rabbit punch) or kidneys · head-butts · using the elbow, forearm or shoulder as a weapon · pushing the opponent's head with the glove · hitting a downed opponent · wrestling, throwing or biting · spitting out the mouthpiece repeatedly · excessive holding. Fouls lead to cautions, point deductions, or disqualification.

### XII.e — How a bout ends

Knockout (KO) · technical knockout (TKO, referee stoppage) · corner stoppage · doctor stoppage · decision (unanimous, split, majority) · draw · no contest · disqualification · retirement.

## Part XIII — The Syllabus: What Goes Into a Class

### Tier 0 — Before you touch anybody (first day)
Hand wrapping and glove fit · warm-up and joint prep · stance and guard · how to hit a bag without hurting the wrist · the rules of safe sparring.

### Tier 1 — Foundations (weeks 1–4, every class)
Stance and weight distribution · step-and-drag footwork · the jab, the cross, the 1–2 · guard returns · basic slip and block · shadowboxing rounds.

### Tier 2 — Power and defense (weeks 4–12)
Lead hook, rear hook, uppercuts · pivots · parry and slip drills · body shots · three- and four-punch combinations · light technical sparring.

### Tier 3 — Counters and angles (weeks 8–20)
Counter to each punch (Part VII) · rolls and weaves · outside-foot control · cutting the ring · mitts with a reactive partner.

### Tier 4 — Strategy (weeks 12+)
Feints and traps · body-first strategy · southpaw vs orthodox · rhythm changes · inside fighting and the clinch.

### Tier 5 — Competition prep
Rules of your ruleset · weight cutting safety (never extreme) · fight-day routine · hard sparring with supervision.

### Tier 6 — Off the mat (forever)
Hand and wrist care · neck training · concussion awareness · sleep and recovery · mental composure.

## Part XIV — Class Templates

### 60-minute class
Warm-up and jump rope (10) · shadowboxing with a theme (10) · technique block (15) · mitts or bag (15) · conditioning (5) · cool-down (5).

## Part XV — Gap Analysis: What The List Above Still Misses

### XV.a — Things missing from almost every syllabus

| Gap | Why it gets left out | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Concussion protocol** | Uncomfortable. | Every gym needs one; sparring volume is the largest controllable risk in the sport. |
| **Defensive habit-forming under fatigue** | Drills are done fresh. | Fights are lost in the 3rd round when the guard drops. |
| **Southpaw preparation** | Most gyms are orthodox. | A southpaw can neutralise an orthodox who never trains against one. |
| **Ring-craft against ropes and corners** | Needs space and a ring. | Where fights are decided. |
| **Cut care and hand health** | Not glamorous. | Prevents shortened careers. |
| **Weight management** | Treated as a secret. | Extreme weight cutting is the greatest cause of avoidable bad performance. |

### XV.b — The honest final gap

The list is not the skill. A boxer who owns the jab, the cross, the left hook, a clean slip, a pivot and a body shot will beat someone with a long list of fancy techniques. **Depth beats breadth. Defense wins long careers. The jab wins rounds.**
