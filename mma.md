# MMA Hub — How the Four Arts Fit Together

> The map that connects **[Jiu-Jitsu](jujitsu.md)**, **[Boxing](boxing.md)**, **[Kickboxing](kickboxing.md)** and **[Muay Thai](muay-thai.md)**. This file does not re-list techniques; it shows how the arts overlap, where the rules change them, and where to learn them in order. Ratings use the same scales as the atlases.

## Part I — The ladder of ranges

An MMA fight moves through five ranges. Each art owns one or two of them and has to be adapted for the rest.

| Range | Who owns it | What happens | Where to look |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Long** | Kickboxing, Muay Thai, boxing | Teeps, obliques, low kicks, jabs, feints | [kickboxing Part V](kickboxing.md), [muay-thai Part VI–VII](muay-thai.md), [boxing Part IV](boxing.md) |
| **Mid (punching)** | Boxing | Straight punches, hooks, counters | [boxing Parts IV–VII](boxing.md) |
| **Short / clinch** | Muay Thai, wrestling, judo | Knees, elbows, collar ties, underhooks, cage pinning | [muay-thai Part X](muay-thai.md), [jujitsu Part II.c](jujitsu.md) |
| **Takedown / takedown defense** | Wrestling, judo, BJJ | Shots, throws, sprawls, cage walls | [jujitsu Parts III–IV](jujitsu.md) |
| **Ground** | Jiu-Jitsu | Positions, passes, escapes, submissions, plus strikes | [jujitsu Parts II, V–IX](jujitsu.md) |

**The MMA rule of thumb:** every transition between ranges is a point of maximum risk. Most fights are decided by who is better at *changing range* on their terms, not by who is best at one range.

## Part II — Same idea, different art (cross-art equivalents)

| Concept | Jiu-Jitsu | Boxing | Kickboxing | Muay Thai |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Distance tool | Grip fighting, collar and sleeve | Jab | Teep, oblique, low kick | Teep |
| Making space | Frame and shrimp | Pivot, push-off in the clinch | Teep, push-off | Teep, frames in the clinch |
| Defending a long weapon | Posture and guard | Slip, parry, pull | Shin check | Shin check, catch |
| Upper-body control | Collar tie, underhook | Tie-up | Single or double collar tie (brief) | Double collar tie (plum) |
| Off-balancing | Sweep | Pivot, shoulder shove | Catch and sweep (where legal) | Sweep, dump |
| Finishing a hurt opponent | Choke | Flurry | Combination | Elbow, knee, flurry |
| Reading intent | Grip and hip cues | Feints, shoulder and eye tells | Feints, knee lift before a kick | Feints, balance shifts |

Each pair is an analogy, not an identity — the *how they differ* is in the atlases.

## Part III — Transitions that only exist in MMA

| Transition | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Strike to shot** | Level change off a punch or kick to enter a takedown | Setups matter; see jujitsu Part III |
| **Sprawl and brawl** | Defend the takedown by sprawling, then strike while standing | Combines [jujitsu Part IV](jujitsu.md) with striking |
| **Clinch to takedown** | Convert a collar tie or underhook into a trip or throw | Cage changes the angles |
| **Cage wrestling** | Using the fence to stand up or to hold an opponent down | Not covered in sport BJJ |
| **Get-up from the ground** | Return to standing under strike threat | See the "wrestling up" gap in [jujitsu Part XV](jujitsu.md) |
| **Ground and pound / defending it** | Strikes from the top; posture, frames and cover from the bottom | Gloves are smaller than in boxing |
| **Takedown defense into striking** | Hip-block, whizzer, sprawl, then re-engage standing | Combines three arts |
| **Back control with strikes** | Hooks plus strikes to force a defensive reaction, then a choke | See [rear naked choke](techniques/submissions/rear-naked-choke.md) |

## Part IV — Ruleset comparison (summary)

Rulesets change what is legal and what scores. Details vary by organisation and change over time — **confirm against the current rulebook before competing.**

| Ruleset | Standing strikes | Clinch | Takedowns | Ground | Typical bout |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Boxing** | Punches only | Brief holding then break | None | None | Up to 12 × 3 min (pro) |
| **K-1 / GLORY kickboxing** | Punches, kicks, knees; no elbows | Brief; one knee then break | Largely banned or limited | None | 3 × 3 min typical |
| **Muay Thai (stadium)** | Punches, kicks, knees, elbows | Extended, scoring | Sweeps and dumps allowed | None | 5 × 3 min |
| **BJJ (IBJJF / ADCC)** | None | Grip and body control | Throws, takedowns, guard pulls | Full | Timed, points or submission |
| **MMA (Unified Rules)** | Punches, kicks, knees, elbows | Full | Full | Full, with strikes | 3 × 5 min; 5 × 5 min for title fights |

**Unified Rules notes:** weight classes run from flyweight (125 lb) to heavyweight (265 lb) in the commonly used set; fouls include eye gouging, groin strikes, head butts, small-joint manipulation, strikes to the back of the head or spine, and certain strikes to a "grounded" opponent. The definition of a grounded opponent and the status of certain elbow strikes have been revised in recent rule updates, so **check the current text** rather than relying on this summary.

## Part V — Mixed learning paths

These paths order material from all four atlases. Each step names the atlas part to study; the training plans schedule the time.

### Path A — Absolute beginner (first 12 weeks)
1. Safety primer: tapping, hand wrapping, sparring ladder (boxing Part XIII Tier 0; jujitsu Part XIII Tier 0)
2. Stance, guard, footwork (boxing Parts II–III)
3. Jab, cross, hook; teep and rear low kick (boxing Part IV, kickboxing Part V)
4. Shrimp, bridge, technical stand-up, breakfalls (jujitsu Part XI)
5. Survive: mount and side-control escapes, turtle (jujitsu Part VII; [escapes library](techniques/escapes/))
6. Basic defense: slips, parries, shin check (boxing Part VI, kickboxing Part VIII)

### Path B — Striker adding grappling
Takedown defense (sprawl, whizzer, front headlock) → get-ups → guard basics → back and mount escapes → one choke from the top (rear naked, arm triangle).

### Path C — Grappler adding striking
Boxing stance and footwork → jab, cross, hook → defense (slip, parry, block) → teep and low kick → clinch posture and collar-tie defense → striking-aware guard (frames against strikes).

### Path D — Competition preparation
Pick the ruleset (Part IV above) → read its rules section in the matching atlas → drill the highest-commonness techniques for that ruleset → supervised hard sparring → weight management plan.

## Part VI — Gap analysis for MMA as a whole

| Gap | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Cage craft** | Fence wrestling and cage-aware striking aren't covered by any single art. |
| **Wrestling as its own art** | Wrestling takedowns and defense are inside the Jiu-Jitsu atlas; a standalone Wrestling atlas is a v2 candidate (see [PRD](docs/PRD.md)). |
| **Judo and sambo throws** | Partly covered under Jiu-Jitsu takedowns. |
| **Fight IQ** | Pacing, scoring awareness and risk judgment don't appear in technique lists. |
| **Concussion and weight-cut management** | Common causes of career damage; need to be taught explicitly. |
| **Women's and weight-class-specific adaptations** | Body-size differences change which techniques work; see the technique library's body-adjustment sections. |
