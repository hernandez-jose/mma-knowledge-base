# Chain Drills (`C-`)

> Nothing works in isolation above white belt. A chain drill trains the thing a
> single-technique drill can't: **their defense is your next entry.**

**How a chain drill runs.** The defending partner gives the *named* defense every
time — not a random one. You attack A, they defend A in the specific way the
chain assumes, you take B. Once the chain is smooth, they get to pick which
defense, and you have to recognise it.

| Stage | Partner's job | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| **Scripted** | Always defend A the same way | 10 laps |
| **Choice** | Pick any listed defense; you react | 3 × 90 s |
| **Live** | Defend freely, from the chain's start position | 2 × 2 min, catch-and-release |

**Catch-and-release is the default finishing rule** for every chain here — lock
it, feel it, let go, keep moving. You are training entries, and entries are what
you're short of.

## C-01 · Armbar ↔ triangle ↔ omoplata

**Start:** closed guard, their posture already broken.

| They defend by… | You take |
|---|---|
| Pulling the trapped arm out and stacking | **Triangle** — their arm left, the other stayed in |
| Posturing up and pulling the head out of the triangle | **Omoplata** — swing the leg over the shoulder |
| Rolling forward out of the omoplata | **Armbar** on the far arm, or follow to the top |

The oldest chain in the sport and still the best first one to own. Every entry
starts from the same broken posture, so the setup work is shared.

- **Cue:** *One of their arms is always in and one is always out.* The chain is just naming which.
- **Pages:** [armbar](../../techniques/submissions/armbar.md) · [triangle](../../techniques/submissions/triangle-choke.md) · [omoplata](../../techniques/submissions/omoplata.md)

## C-02 · RNC ↔ collar choke ↔ armbar

**Start:** back control, seatbelt, both hooks.

| They defend by… | You take |
|---|---|
| Two-on-one on your choking hand | **Collar choke** (gi) or switch sides and re-seatbelt (no-gi) |
| Defending high with both hands and turning in | **Armbar** on the exposed arm as they turn |
| Clearing a hook and flattening you | **Body triangle**, re-establish, restart |

- **Cue:** *Threaten the neck to harvest the arm.* Nobody gives you an arm until their neck is in danger.
- **Pages:** [RNC](../../techniques/submissions/rear-naked-choke.md) · [bow and arrow](../../techniques/submissions/bow-and-arrow.md) · [armbar](../../techniques/submissions/armbar.md)

## C-03 · Kimura ↔ guillotine ↔ back take

**Start:** kimura grip from half guard bottom, or from a front headlock.

| They defend by… | You take |
|---|---|
| Straightening the arm and posting | **Guillotine** as their head comes down |
| Pulling the head out and driving over | **Back take** — the kimura grip walks them past your hips |
| Rolling to escape the grip | **Sweep** with the grip, come up on top |

The kimura grip is a *control*, not just a submission — which is the actual
lesson of this drill.

- **Pages:** [kimura](../../techniques/submissions/kimura.md) · [guillotine](../../techniques/submissions/guillotine.md)

## C-04 · Knee cut ↔ back step ↔ leg drag

**Start:** headquarters position (your knee across their near leg, their far foot on your hip).

| They defend by… | You take |
|---|---|
| Framing hard and getting the underhook | **Back step** to the other side |
| Blocking your hip with the far knee | **Leg drag** — drag the far leg across your centre |
| Recovering their frames and turning in | Back to **headquarters** and reset |

Headquarters is a hub, not a position — that's why passes are drilled as a chain
and never as three separate techniques.

## C-05 · Toreando ↔ leg drag ↔ back exposure

**Start:** standing at their feet, both legs controlled.

Pin the legs to one side (toreando) → they turn in to recover → **leg drag** their
top leg across → they turn away to escape the drag → **take the back**.

- **Cue:** *Every pass they escape moves them one step closer to giving you the back.* The pass and the back take are the same movement in different amounts.

## C-06 · Scissor ↔ hip bump ↔ kimura sweep

**Start:** closed guard, cross-collar or collar-and-sleeve grip.

| They defend by… | You take |
|---|---|
| Basing wide against the scissor | **Hip bump** — sit up as they post back |
| Posting the hand back to stop the hip bump | **Kimura sweep** on that posted arm |
| Ripping the arm free | **Scissor** again — they've given the space back |

Three sweeps, one grip, one chain. This is the closed-guard equivalent of C-01.

## C-07 · Butterfly elevator ↔ arm drag ↔ back

**Start:** butterfly guard, both hooks in, one underhook.

Elevator sweep to the underhook side → they post out wide → **arm drag** the far
arm → they turn away → **back take** with the hook already in.

- **Cue:** *Elevate to make them post; drag the post.*

## C-08 · Snap-down ↔ front headlock ↔ finish

**Start:** standing, collar tie.

Snap them down → front headlock → then, by their reaction:

| They react by… | You take |
|---|---|
| Staying low and square | **Guillotine** |
| Driving the far arm in | **D'Arce** |
| Keeping the near arm in | **Anaconda** |
| Standing up | **Back take** |
| Retreating their hips | **Go-behind** |

The highest-return chain in no-gi and MMA, because it starts from the thing that
happens every time somebody's takedown fails.

- **Pairs with:** [P-14](partner-drills.md#p-14--the-choke-map-circuit) and [G-07](positional-games.md#g-07--front-headlock-game)

## C-09 · Single leg ↔ high crotch ↔ go-behind

**Start:** on a single leg, head inside or outside.

| They defend by… | You take |
|---|---|
| Whizzer and hips back | **Go-behind** — dump the leg, run the hip |
| Limp-legging out | **High crotch** on the same side, re-shoot |
| Sprawling heavy | **Sit-out** and come up behind |

- **Cue:** *A takedown that fails should end behind them, not in front of them.*

## C-10 · Mount escape ↔ half guard ↔ wrestle-up

**Start:** bottom mount.

Elbow-knee escape → half guard bottom → knee shield and underhook → come up to
the dogfight → wrestle up to standing or finish the sweep.

- **Why it's a chain:** most students learn the mount escape and stop, arriving in half guard with no plan. The escape isn't finished until you're on top or on your feet.

## C-11 · Ankle lock ↔ extraction ↔ pass

**Start:** you in outside ashi with their leg.

Threaten the straight ankle lock → they defend by turning away → **extract your
leg** as they turn → **pass** to side control on the far side.

- **Cue:** *The leg entanglement is a passing position that happens to contain a submission.* Treating it that way keeps you safe and keeps you scoring.
- **Safety:** straight ankle lock only in this chain. Rotational attacks are coach-supervised, slow, and defense-first. See [G-11](positional-games.md#g-11--leg-lock-defense-only).

## C-12 · The pin ladder

**Start:** side control, partner at 40%.

Side control → they turn away → **north–south** → they bridge → **knee-on-belly**
→ they frame → **mount** → they turn in → **technical mount** → they keep turning
→ **back control**.

Every transition is triggered by *their* movement. That's the entire lesson: you
don't choose the next pin, they do.

- **Pairs with:** [P-12](partner-drills.md#p-12--pin-transition-circuit) and [G-04](positional-games.md#g-04--hold-the-pin)

## Building your own chain

A chain is worth drilling when all three are true:

1. **Shared setup** — the entries come from the same grip or the same broken posture. Two techniques that need different setups aren't a chain, they're a list.
2. **Their defense is the trigger** — you shouldn't have to decide; the door opens itself.
3. **Three links, maximum.** Longer chains are demonstrations, not skills. Nobody has ever hit link six.
