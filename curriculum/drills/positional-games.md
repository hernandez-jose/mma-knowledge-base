# Positional Games (`G-`)

> Live rounds with a constraint and a score. This is rung 4 — where a motor
> pattern becomes a skill you can actually *find* under pressure, and where
> training stops being homework and starts being a contest you want to settle.

**The format, every time:**

1. **Start position** — exact, both players placed before the whistle.
2. **Win condition** — one for each player, stated out loud.
3. **Reset** — on a score, or on the clock. **90 seconds is the default.**
4. **Swap** — always run both roles. A game you only play from the top teaches half a lesson.

Positional sparring produces roughly **three times the reps** of the relevant
situation compared with open rolling. It is the highest-value fifteen minutes in
any class, and it's the first thing cut when a coach talks too long.

## G-01 · Escape or die

**Start:** rotating — mount, side control, back control, knee-on-belly, north–south.
**Bottom wins:** escape to guard, turtle-facing, or a better position.
**Top wins:** hold for the full 90 s, or advance up the ladder.

Four rounds, one from each bad position, no rest between. This is the single
most valuable game in this file, and the one students avoid.

- **Coach note:** the top player's job is to be *honest*, not to win. Riding a beginner at 100% teaches them nothing except that they hate Tuesdays.
- **Feeds:** [Module 02](../modules/02-survival.md) · escape pages in [techniques/escapes](../../techniques/escapes/)

## G-02 · Guard retention king of the hill

**Start:** bottom seated or supine, top standing at their feet.
**Bottom wins:** survive 60 s without being passed, or sweep.
**Top wins:** pass and hold 3 s — then they stay in and the next passer comes on.

Line up three or four passers. The bottom player stays until they're passed.

- **Constraint that makes it work:** the bottom player may **not** close the guard. Retention only.
- **Feeds:** [Module 04](../modules/04-guard.md)

## G-03 · Pass or sweep

**Start:** top player inside the bottom player's guard, hands on the mat.
**Top wins:** pass and control 3 s.
**Bottom wins:** sweep and control 3 s, or submit.

The purest game in jiu-jitsu, and the one to default to when you have fifteen
minutes and no plan.

- **Variation:** no submissions for either player. Forces the actual positional battle.
- **Feeds:** Modules [04](../modules/04-guard.md), [05](../modules/05-sweeps.md), [06](../modules/06-passing.md)

## G-04 · Hold the pin

**Start:** top in side control, bottom flat.
**Top wins:** hold any legal pin for 20 continuous seconds.
**Bottom wins:** recover guard or reverse.

Twenty seconds is a long time when someone is actively escaping. Top players
learn that a pin is a *sequence* of pins, not a hold.

- **Progression:** top must transition pins at least twice during the round. No camping.
- **Feeds:** [Module 07](../modules/07-pins-and-transitions.md)

## G-05 · Back attack / back defense

**Start:** attacker with the seatbelt and both hooks in, defender's hands down.
**Attacker wins:** any choke.
**Defender wins:** clear both hooks and get their shoulders to the mat, or turn in.

Ninety seconds, then swap. This game teaches both the highest-percentage
attacking position in the sport and the most common way beginners lose.

- **Detail pages:** [RNC](../../techniques/submissions/rear-naked-choke.md) · [back escapes](../../techniques/escapes/back-escapes.md)

## G-06 · Turtle wars

**Start:** one player turtled, the other with a seatbelt-side ride.
**Turtled player wins:** face them (sit-out), stand up, or recover guard.
**Rider wins:** take the back with both hooks, or flatten them out.

- **Why it exists:** everybody ends up in turtle, and untrained it's a free back-take donation for your entire white-belt career.
- **Feeds:** [Module 10](../modules/10-scrambles.md)

## G-07 · Front headlock game

**Start:** one player in a front headlock, the other on their knees with their head under.
**Top wins:** any front headlock finish, a back take, or a go-behind.
**Bottom wins:** stand up facing them, or get a leg and come up.

- **Pairs with:** [P-14 choke map circuit](partner-drills.md#p-14--the-choke-map-circuit) and [C-08](chain-drills.md#c-08--snap-down--front-headlock--finish).
- **Feeds:** Modules [08](../modules/08-submissions.md), [10](../modules/10-scrambles.md)

## G-08 · Wrestle-up

**Start:** bottom player seated, top player standing, one grip each.
**Bottom wins:** come up to standing with control, or complete a takedown.
**Top wins:** flatten them, pass, or sprawl them back to their seat.

The highest-scoring bottom action in modern no-gi, and the self-defense answer
to being on the floor: **get up**.

- **Feeds:** Modules [05](../modules/05-sweeps.md), [09](../modules/09-takedowns.md)

## G-09 · Grips only

**Start:** standing (or both seated).
**Both win by:** establishing a named dominant grip and holding it 3 seconds.
No takedowns, no passing, no submissions.

Named grips: **gi** — cross collar, collar-and-sleeve, two-on-one, sleeve-and-pant.
**No-gi** — collar tie, underhook, two-on-one, wrist control, bodylock.

- **Dose:** four 60-second rounds. Do this every week forever.
- **Feeds:** [Module 11](../modules/11-concepts.md)

## G-10 · Scramble start

**Start:** deliberately broken — both players on hands and knees head-to-head; or
one player's shot caught halfway with the other in a half-sprawl; or both in a
double-underhook stand-off on the mat.
**Both win by:** establishing any scoring position and holding 3 s.

Rounds of 20 seconds, reset immediately, ten of them. Short and violent by
design — a scramble is decided in the first three seconds.

- **Why it exists:** more matches are decided in transitional chaos than in named positions, and almost nobody trains it because it can't be demonstrated cleanly.
- **Feeds:** [Module 10](../modules/10-scrambles.md)

## G-11 · Leg lock defense only

**Start:** partner has outside ashi garami with your leg in, at 30%.
**Defender wins:** hide the heel, clear the knee line, extract and come on top.
**Attacker wins:** achieve a *controlled* straight ankle lock position — no rotational attacks, no finishing pressure.

- **Rule:** defense-only for the first eight weeks a student meets leg locks. Beginners meet heel hooks at open mats long before their belt allows them; teaching defense first is a safety measure, not a tactical one.
- **Feeds:** [Module 08](../modules/08-submissions.md)

## G-12 · First grip to first takedown

**Start:** standing, out of range.
**Both win by:** any takedown landing in guard or better, held 3 s. Guard pulls score nothing.

Sixty-second rounds. Soft mats or crash pads. Tired legs make bad shots — put
this early in the session, not late.

- **Variation ("touch-and-go"):** no finishing the takedown; score for getting behind them or lifting a leg clear of the mat. Far safer, nearly as useful.
- **Feeds:** [Module 09](../modules/09-takedowns.md)

## G-13 · Handicap games

**Start:** any of the above, with one player constrained.

| Handicap | What it forces |
|---|---|
| **One arm in the belt** | Grip economy; kills the strength answer |
| **No hands for the top player** | Real weight distribution instead of posting |
| **Bottom player starts flat, arms crossed** | Frames before movement |
| **Higher belt may only defend** | Their escapes; the beginner's offense |
| **No submissions** | Position, which was the point |

- **Coach note:** handicaps are how a 100 kg purple belt and a 60 kg white belt get a *useful* round instead of a polite one.

## G-14 · Submission escape only

**Start:** the submission already applied at ~70% — armbar half-extended, triangle
locked but not squeezed, RNC with the grip in but no pressure.
**Defender wins:** escape to a neutral or better position.
**Attacker wins:** finish.

Run one submission per round: armbar, triangle, RNC, guillotine, kimura, arm
triangle.

- **Rule:** the attacker applies pressure at a **crawl**. This game only works if nobody gets hurt in it.
- **Feeds:** [Module 03](../modules/03-submission-defense.md)

## G-15 · Half guard, both chairs

**Start:** bottom player with a knee shield, top player with a crossface.
**Bottom wins:** sweep, come up, or recover full guard.
**Top wins:** pass to side control and hold 3 s.

Half guard is where most people leak position, and it's the position both an MMA
fighter and a 50-year-old hobbyist will spend the most time in.

- **Feeds:** Modules [04](../modules/04-guard.md), [06](../modules/06-passing.md)

## G-16 · Knee-on-belly clock

**Start:** top player with knee on belly, hands off.
**Top wins:** hold 15 s, or transition to mount/back.
**Bottom wins:** escape or recover guard.

Miserable and short, which is the point — knee-on-belly is the position that
teaches breathing under discomfort faster than anything else.

- **Detail page:** [knee-on-belly escapes](../../techniques/escapes/knee-on-belly-escapes.md)

## G-17 · The ladder game

**Start:** both players on their knees, hands touching.
**Scoring:** [`jujitsu.md` Part I](../../jujitsu.md#part-i--the-map) positional values. Each time you improve a rung and hold 3 s, call the number out loud. Highest total in 3 minutes wins.

Saying the number out loud is the whole drill — it forces you to think in
positions rather than in techniques, which is the mental model the entire sport
is built on.

- **Feeds:** Modules [07](../modules/07-pins-and-transitions.md), [11](../modules/11-concepts.md)

## Picking a game

| If the class just learned… | Run this |
|---|---|
| An escape | G-01, G-14 |
| A guard | G-02, G-03, G-15 |
| A sweep | G-03, G-08 |
| A pass | G-03, G-15, G-02 |
| A pin or transition | G-04, G-16, G-17 |
| A submission | G-05, G-07, G-14 |
| A takedown | G-12, G-09 |
| Nothing — it's an open mat | G-03, then G-10 |
