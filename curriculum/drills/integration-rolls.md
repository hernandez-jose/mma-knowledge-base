# Integration Rolls (`R-`)

> **This is the file the whole curriculum points at.**
>
> Everything else in this library is preparation. The roll is where it either
> becomes yours or quietly stays a thing you can demonstrate. These are the roll
> formats that fold new material into live sparring instead of letting open
> rolling wash it away.

---

## The problem this file solves

Here is what happens in almost every gym, every week:

> Class teaches a technique. Students drill it twenty times. The whistle goes for
> open rolling. Everybody immediately plays their existing game, hits none of
> today's material, and forgets it inside a fortnight.

Open rolling is not where you learn — it's where you **audit**. Under real
resistance you default to what you already own, which is precisely the material
you don't need reps of. New material dies in open rolling unless something
forces it into the round.

**Every format below is a way of forcing it in.** Pick one per session. The
constraint comes off once the technique starts appearing on its own — which is
the definition of having learned it.

## R-01 · Flow roll

**Rules:** 40–50%. Continuous movement, no stalling, no finishing. Positions are
given up freely — when someone earns a position, take it, then let the round keep
moving. Nobody wins.

**What it's for:** volume. A flow round contains three to four times the
positional transitions of a hard round, which makes it the best possible place to
rehearse *sequences* rather than moments.

- **The trap:** flow rolling with someone who doesn't know how is just losing slowly. Both people must agree to the number out loud first.
- **Use it:** taper weeks, day after a competition, rolling with a brand-new person, or as the last round of any session.

## R-02 · Task roll

**Rules:** 70%. Each player gets **one** task, stated out loud before the round.
Everything else is a normal roll. You win the round by completing your task —
not by tapping your partner.

| Example task | Assigned to |
|---|---|
| "Get to mount three times, however you like" | The person who learned mount today |
| "Every time you're passed, recover guard within 15 seconds" | The retention student |
| "Finish exactly one submission, and it must be a choke" | The joint-lock addict |
| "Take the back from turtle at least once" | The student who avoids turtle |
| "Don't use your right arm" | The one who muscles everything |

**This is the default format of this curriculum.** Every module in
[`modules/`](../modules/) ends with a "blending it into a roll" section, and what
it gives you is a list of tasks to use here.

- **Coach note:** tasks must be *achievable in one round*. "Hit a berimbolo" isn't a task, it's a wish.

## R-03 · Handicap roll

**Rules:** 70–90%. One player is constrained so the round is genuinely
competitive for both.

| Handicap | Who it's for |
|---|---|
| Starts in a bad position every restart | The much better player |
| One arm in the belt | The much stronger player |
| Defense only — may not attack, only escape and survive | The higher belt |
| No submissions; positions only | The person who skips position |
| Must announce the submission before applying it | The sneaky one |

- **Why it matters:** a handicap turns a useless round into two useful ones. The lower belt gets a real fight; the higher belt gets forced into their weak areas.

## R-04 · Positional restart roll

**Rules:** 80%. Normal rolling, but the coach (or a timer) resets both players to
a named position every 60–90 seconds regardless of what's happening. Reset
positions rotate through the ladder: guard, half, side, mount, back, standing.

**What it's for:** it removes the stalling that lets people avoid their weak
positions for an entire round, and it's the closest simulation of the way a
competition match actually feels — repeated hard restarts, never a settled game.

## R-05 · Chain roll

**Rules:** 70%, catch-and-release. You may not attack the same submission twice
in a row — every attack must be a **response** to their defense of the previous
one. Lock it, feel it, release, take the next link.

**What it's for:** the fastest way to convert [chain drills](chain-drills.md)
into live skill, and the safest way to roll hard on submissions.

- **Scoring, if you want one:** longest chain of the round wins. Three links is a good round; four is a very good one.

## R-06 · Breathing roll

**Rules:** 60%, **nasal breathing only**. If your mouth opens, you stop, sit up,
and recover your breathing before continuing. Six minutes, no reset.

**What it's for:** panic is roughly 80% oxygen debt. Most people who say they
have a cardio problem have a *breathing* problem — they hold their breath under
pressure, spike their heart rate, and then blame their conditioning.

- **Pair with:** [S-15](solo-drills.md#s-15--breathing-ladder).
- **Best partner:** somebody 20 kg heavier who will sit on you. That's the point.

## R-07 · Shark tank

**Rules:** 100%. One player stays in; fresh partners enter every 90 seconds for
five to eight rounds.

**What it's for:** competition preparation, and only that. It's a conditioning
and composure test, not a learning environment — you cannot acquire skill while
this tired.

- **Frequency:** peak weeks only. Once a fortnight at most, never in the last week before a competition.
- **Do not:** put a beginner in the tank. It's how people get hurt and quit.

## R-08 · King of the hill

**Rules:** 80%. Winner stays on. Rounds are 2 minutes or until a submission or a
clean positional score, whichever is first. Losers rotate to the back of the line.

**What it's for:** energy. Large classes, high engagement, and the closest thing
training has to a scoreboard. This is the format people stay late for.

- **Variation:** run it from a position rather than standing — king of the hill from guard, from back control, from a scramble start.

## R-09 · Broken-position roll

**Rules:** 80%. Every restart begins from deliberately unstable positions —
a caught shot, a half-completed pass, both players turtled head-to-head, a failed
sweep with both on their sides. 30-second rounds, immediate resets, ten of them.

**What it's for:** the scramble, which decides more matches than any named
position and which almost nobody trains because it can't be demonstrated cleanly.

- **Pairs with:** [G-10](positional-games.md#g-10--scramble-start).

## R-10 · Blindfold start

**Rules:** 60%. Both players close their eyes (or one player wears a blindfold)
for the first 30 seconds of each round from a grip-established start. Then eyes
open and the round continues normally.

**What it's for:** jiu-jitsu is a tactile sport. Removing sight forces you to
read weight, pressure and grip — which is the information you actually navigate
by once someone is chest-to-chest with you.

- **Safety:** never blindfolded while standing. Ground starts only, and never near the wall or another pair.

## R-11 · Scored roll

**Rules:** 85%, with a third person refereeing under [IBJJF
scoring](../../jujitsu.md#ibjjf-points). Real 5-minute match, real points, real
advantages, called out loud. Referee explains every call afterwards.

**What it's for:** most competitors don't know why they lost. Rolling under a
scoring system a few weeks before an event is the difference between competing
and just showing up.

- **Rotate the referee's role.** Learning to score a match teaches you to read your own.

## R-12 · Body mismatch roll

**Rules:** vary with the gap. Not a game — a protocol for the rounds that make up
most of everybody's training.

| Gap | The rule |
|---|---|
| **They're 30 kg heavier** | They don't use weight as a technique; you don't muscle out of anything. Frames and timing only. Bottom player calls the pace. |
| **They're 30 kg lighter** | You're a training dummy with opinions. Move, defend, create problems — never crush. Your job is to lose well and make them work. |
| **They're brand new** | No submissions from you. Let them work, guide them out loud, tap early if they stumble into something. |
| **They're twice your skill** | Ask for one thing before the round: "hold me in side control, I'm working escapes." A good higher belt will give you exactly that. |
| **They're 25 years older** | Slow entries, no explosive scrambles, no stacking, tap early on both sides. |

- **Why this is a numbered format:** how to roll with different bodies is assumed to be obvious, taught nowhere, and getting it wrong is one of the main reasons people get hurt and quit.

## R-13 · Strikes-aware roll

**Rules:** 60–80% grappling with **open-palm touches to the head** allowed at
light contact, or with one player holding focus mitts. Head contact is a *tag*,
not a strike — it exists to inform position, not to hurt.

**What it's for:** sport jiu-jitsu has drifted a long way from fighting. Ten
minutes of this reorders your entire game: closed guard gets better, inverted
guard gets worse, posture control becomes urgent, and standing up stops being
optional.

- **Requires:** a partner you trust and mouthguards. Do not improvise this with strangers.
- **The full version:** [`training-plan.md`](../../training-plan.md) — the 8-week MMA camp.

## R-14 · Ladder roll

**Rules:** 80%. You may only attack a submission from **+2 or better** on the
[positional ladder](../../jujitsu.md#part-i--the-map) — side control, mount,
back, north–south, knee-on-belly. Submissions from guard don't count. Announce
the rung when you arrive.

**What it's for:** *position before submission* stops being a slogan and becomes
a rule with a scoreboard. It's also the single fastest fix for the student who
dives on every armbar and loses every position.

- **Reverse version (advanced):** guard attacks only. Nothing from the top. Forces a complete bottom game.

## R-15 · Debrief roll

**Rules:** any format, plus **three lines written down** at the end:

1. What worked.
2. What didn't.
3. What to ask about next class.

**What it's for:** note-taking after training roughly doubles retention, costs
ninety seconds, and almost nobody does it. If you adopt exactly one thing from
this entire curriculum, adopt this one.

- **Do it with your partner**, out loud, before you write. They saw the round from the inside — they know why your pass failed better than you do.

## How to blend a module into your rolling

The four-week arc that takes new material from "shown in class" to "part of my
game." Run it for any module in [`modules/`](../modules/):

| Week | Roll format | Constraint level | What you should see |
|:-:|---|---|---|
| **1** | [R-02 task roll](#r-02--task-roll) | Heavy — the task is the only thing that counts | The technique appears because you forced it |
| **2** | [R-05 chain roll](#r-05--chain-roll) or [R-04 restart roll](#r-04--positional-restart-roll) | Medium — start position given, choices free | It appears when the position is handed to you |
| **3** | Open rolling, **one** task kept | Light | It appears two or three times a round without a reminder |
| **4** | Open rolling, no constraint | None | It appears when you weren't thinking about it — **it's yours** |

If week 4 comes and the technique never shows up unprompted, it hasn't been
learned; it's been demonstrated. Go back to week 1 with it, or drop it and pick
something that fits your game better. Both are honest answers. Pretending is not.

## Rolling etiquette (state these once, keep them forever)

- **Call the percentage before the round.** Most injuries are two people rolling at different numbers.
- **Declare injuries before, not during.**
- **Tap early, release instantly.** Releasing fast is the higher skill.
- **Slow down for the new person and the returning-from-injury person.** Every time.
- **The round ends when someone says it ends.** No "one more" after a tap that felt bad.
- **You are allowed to say no to a roll.** No explanation owed, ever.
- **Losing is the tuition.** The number one reason people quit in their first six months is that nobody told them how much losing the first year contains.
