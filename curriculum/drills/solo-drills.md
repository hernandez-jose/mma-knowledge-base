# Solo Drills (`S-`)

> The physical alphabet. No partner, no mat fee, no excuse. These are the
> movements every technique in the atlas is spelled with — cheap to practice,
> expensive to lack.
>
> Atlas reference: [`jujitsu.md` Part XI — Solo Movements](../../jujitsu.md#part-xi--solo-movements-the-physical-alphabet)

**How to use these:** three or four of them, chosen to rehearse the day's
technique, is a complete warm-up. Ten minutes of them at home on a rest day is
the single highest-return thing a beginner can do off the mat.

**The standard length:** one "length" = one width of a normal mat, roughly 10 m.
If you're at home, 8–10 reps is a length.

## S-01 · Shrimp ladder

**Trains:** the hip escape · **Dose:** 2 lengths each direction · **Feeds:** Modules 01, 02, 04

On your back, heels close to your hips. Bridge slightly onto one shoulder, turn
to that side, and push your hips *backwards* away from where the pressure would
be, framing with both arms as if pushing a chest off you. Land on your side, not
flat. Reset and go the other way.

- **Cue:** *Hips travel, shoulders stay.* Your head should barely move down the mat.
- **Fail signal:** you're sliding on your shoulder blades — that's a wiggle, not an escape.
- **Progression:** do it with your hands framing an imaginary opponent at neck and hip, never below your own chest line.

## S-02 · Reverse shrimp ladder

**Trains:** closing distance and re-guarding · **Dose:** 2 lengths each direction · **Feeds:** Modules 01, 04

The shrimp travelling forwards. From your side, post the top foot, drive your
hips *towards* the imaginary opponent and turn onto the other side. This is how
you chase a passer's hips instead of letting them walk around you.

- **Cue:** *Chase the hip, not the head.*
- **Fail signal:** you sit up to travel. Stay on your side; the shoulder stays low.

## S-03 · Bridge series

**Trains:** the upa and every escape built on it · **Dose:** 10 straight, 10 each side · **Feeds:** Modules 01, 02

Three variants, in order. **(a) Straight bridge:** heels close, drive hips to the
ceiling, weight into your feet and upper back — never the crown of your head.
**(b) Bridge and turn:** bridge, then finish over one shoulder so you end on your
knees or on your side. **(c) Bridge and shrimp:** bridge, turn, shrimp out — the
actual escape sequence.

- **Cue:** *Bridge into them, not just up.* Vertical lift moves nobody.
- **Fail signal:** your heels slide away as you lift — they should stay glued near your hips.
- **Safety:** no neck bridges (weight on the head) unless a coach has built you up to them over months.

## S-04 · Technical stand-up on the whistle

**Trains:** getting off the floor facing the threat · **Dose:** 15 reps, alternating posting sides · **Feeds:** Modules 01, 02, 10

Sit up on one hip. Post the same-side hand behind you and the opposite foot in
front. Kick the posted-side leg through underneath, come up with your posting
hand leaving the mat *last*, finishing in a fighting stance facing forward.

- **Cue:** *Never turn your back to stand.* Also the single most important self-defense movement in the sport.
- **Fail signal:** you stand facing sideways, or your hand stays down after your hips are up.

## S-05 · Shoulder rolls

**Trains:** rolling without loading the neck · **Dose:** 6 forward, 6 backward, each shoulder · **Feeds:** Modules 01, 10

Forward: reach one arm across your body, tuck the chin to the *opposite*
shoulder, and roll diagonally from shoulder to opposite hip. Backward: the same
line in reverse, looking over the shoulder you're rolling on.

- **Cue:** *Over the shoulder, never over the head.*
- **Fail signal:** you feel it in your neck, or you land flat on your back with a thud.

## S-06 · Breakfall ladder

**Trains:** taking a throw without an injury · **Dose:** 10 side each way, 10 back · **Feeds:** Modules 01, 09

**Side breakfall:** from a squat, extend one leg across, sit down onto the hip and
slap the mat with the whole arm at 45°, chin tucked, exhaling hard.
**Back breakfall:** sit, roll back, both arms slap, chin to chest.

- **Cue:** *Slap and exhale on the same beat.* The exhale is not optional — it's what stops the wind being knocked out.
- **Fail signal:** your head touches the mat, or you slap with a cupped hand.
- **Why it matters:** the best injury-prevention skill in grappling, and the only one that transfers to icy pavements.

## S-07 · Sprawl ladder

**Trains:** the takedown-defense reflex · **Dose:** 3 × 10 reps · **Feeds:** Modules 01, 09

From a fighting stance, drop your hips to the mat, shoot both legs back and wide,
chest forward and heavy, head up. Recover to stance immediately — the recovery
is half the drill.

- **Cue:** *Hips punch the mat first.* If your feet move before your hips, you're jumping, not sprawling.
- **Progression:** sprawl → circle to the side and face → sprawl again. Reflex plus reposition.

## S-08 · Sit-through / hip heist

**Trains:** turning your hips through from a base · **Dose:** 10 each side · **Feeds:** Modules 01, 10

From a bear-crawl base, post one hand, kick the opposite leg through under your
body and turn your hips to face the ceiling, landing on the outside hip. This is
the escape hatch from turtle and the recovery from a failed shot.

- **Cue:** *Post, kick, face them.*
- **Fail signal:** you rise onto your knees before turning — that hands your back over.

## S-09 · Granby roll

**Trains:** inverted escape and guard retention · **Dose:** 5 each direction, slowly · **Feeds:** Modules 01, 04, 10

From your side, roll over your upper back and shoulder — hips lifting over your
head — and come out facing the other way. Build this slowly.

- **Cue:** *Weight on the shoulder blade, never the crown of the head.*
- **Safety:** neck-loading movement. If you have any neck history, do S-10 for a month before this.

## S-10 · Inversion build-up

**Trains:** neck-safe inverting · **Dose:** 3 × 8, controlled · **Feeds:** Modules 01, 04

Lie on your back, roll your hips up over your head until your knees are near your
ears, weight on the upper back and shoulders, then lower with control. Add a
quarter turn each session once it's comfortable.

- **Cue:** *Shoulders take the load; the head floats.*
- **Fail signal:** any pinching in the neck. Stop for the day; volume is what builds this, not intensity.

## S-11 · Penetration step reps

**Trains:** the takedown entry · **Dose:** 10 each side, mirror then on a bag · **Feeds:** Modules 01, 09

From stance, change level with the **knees** (not the waist), step the lead foot
deep between their feet with the knee over the toes, head and chest up, then
drive the back leg through and come up.

- **Cue:** *Level change first, step second.* Bending at the waist is how you get guillotined.
- **Fail signal:** your head drops below your hips at any point.

## S-12 · Guard retention shadow

**Trains:** leg pummelling and hip switching · **Dose:** 3 × 45 s · **Feeds:** Module 04

On your back, feet in the air. Pummel one leg inside then the other (as if
re-inserting a hook), switch your hips side to side, insert an imaginary knee
shield, recover to a square guard. Continuous, no pauses.

- **Cue:** *Your legs are arms.* Retention is a pummelling skill, not a strength one.
- **Progression:** add a shrimp between each pummel so you're moving in space, not just cycling in place.

## S-13 · Shadow wrestling

**Trains:** stance, motion and level change · **Dose:** 3 × 2 min · **Feeds:** Module 09

Continuous movement in a fighting stance: circle, change levels, fake a shot,
snap an imaginary head down, sprawl, re-stance. Keep the hands working the whole
time — this is a grip-fighting drill as much as a footwork one.

- **Cue:** *Feet quiet, hands busy.*
- **Progression:** call your own sequence out loud. Naming it while doing it doubles retention.

## S-14 · Neck, grip and hip prehab

**Trains:** the ability to still be training in ten years · **Dose:** 2–3 × per week, 10 min · **Feeds:** Module 13

Four items, circuit style. **Neck:** isometric holds in four directions, 20 s each,
plus banded flexion/extension — this is concussion and crank resilience.
**Grip:** dead hangs and towel hangs, 3 × 30 s; finger extensor band work to
balance all the gripping. **Hips:** 90/90 switches, deep squat holds, couch
stretch. **Rotational core:** cable or band chops, 3 × 12 each way.

- **Cue:** *Tape your fingers before they hurt, not after.*
- **Why it's here:** BJJ finger arthritis is near-universal and neck injuries end careers. This is the least glamorous drill in the library and the one you'll be most glad you did.

## S-15 · Breathing ladder

**Trains:** composure under load · **Dose:** 5 min, daily is better than long · **Feeds:** Modules 00, 11

Nasal only. Inhale 4 counts, exhale 6. Then do it again in a bad position: lie
under a heavy bag or a weight plate on your chest, or hold a deep squat, and keep
the same rhythm for 90 seconds.

- **Cue:** *Panic is mostly oxygen debt.* Fixing your breathing beats a year of conditioning.
- **Progression:** the live version is [R-06 breathing roll](integration-rolls.md#r-06--breathing-roll).
