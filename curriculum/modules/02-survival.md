## Why this module exists

New students want submissions. What actually keeps them in the sport is not
being crushed. A student who can survive gets to *train* — they last the round,
they learn from better partners instead of just enduring them, and they stop
spending every roll in a panic.

There's a harder argument too: **escapes are the only skill that never becomes
optional.** You will change guards, abandon passes and fall out of love with
submissions. You will need the upa and the frame-and-shrimp at seventy.

Depth beats breadth here more than anywhere. Two escapes per position, owned
completely, beat eight you can name.

| **Kesa gatame** | Free the trapped arm, bridge to the head side, turn out | [kesa-gatame-escapes.md](../../techniques/escapes/kesa-gatame-escapes.md) |

Plus the thing underneath all of them: **defensive posture** — elbows in, hands
protecting the neck, chin tucked, knees active, never flat.

## Lessons

| # | Lesson | Content | Game |
|:-:|---|---|---|
| **2.1** | Defensive posture and frames | Bone structures, not muscle. Forearm and shin frames from under mount, side control and KOB. **Frames are how small people survive big people.** | G-01 |
| **2.2** | Mount escapes | Upa when their weight is forward, elbow-knee when it's back. Bridge first, *feel* which, then choose. | G-01 |
| **2.3** | Side control escapes | Elbow to knee, knee to elbow. Frame short and bone-on-bone. The underhook option when they're heavy on the far side. | G-01 |
| **2.4** | Back defense | Chin down, two hands on the choking hand, shoulders to the mat on the non-choking side, clear the hook, turn in. | G-05 |
| **2.5** | Knee-on-belly and north–south | The two positions nobody drills escaping and everybody hates being in. | G-16 |
| **2.6** | Turtle, both ways | How to get there safely, and three ways out before they attach. | G-06 |
| **2.7** | Escaping *early* | The whole module in one idea: every escape is easy one second before it's needed and nearly impossible five seconds after. Escape the *transition*, not the pin. | G-01 |

## Blending it into a roll

Tasks for [R-02](../drills/integration-rolls.md#r-02--task-roll):

- "Start every round in a bad position of your partner's choosing."
- "Escape mount three times this round, however you like."
- "Recover guard within 15 seconds of being passed, every time."
- "You may not attack anything for the first two minutes."
- "Never be flat on your back — if you land flat, get to your side before your next action."

**The four-week arc:** week 1 give away the position deliberately; week 2 run
[R-04 restart rolls](../drills/integration-rolls.md#r-04--positional-restart-roll) with the
resets in bad positions; week 3 roll open with one escape task; week 4 roll open
and count how many times your escapes appeared without a reminder.

## Checkpoint

- [ ] Escape mount at 70% within 60 s, using the correct escape for their weight
- [ ] Escape side control at 70% within 60 s, both the framing and the underhook route
- [ ] Escape back control at 70% — hand fight first, then the slide
- [ ] Survive 2 minutes under a partner 20 kg heavier without panicking or going flat
- [ ] Exit turtle three different ways against a rider
- [ ] Breathe nasally for a full round spent entirely on the bottom

## Common errors

| Error | Fix |
|---|---|
| Long frames with locked elbows | Frames live inside your own chest line, bone-on-bone. A straight arm is a gift-wrapped armbar. |
| Bridging straight up | Bridge *into* them and over one shoulder. Vertical lift moves nobody. |
| Escaping the pin instead of the transition | Start moving while they're travelling, not once they've settled. |
| Turning away to escape | Turning away is how backs are given. Turn *in*, or come to your side facing them. |
| Waiting to be rescued by the round ending | Survival is active. Frame, breathe, move — in that order, continuously. |
