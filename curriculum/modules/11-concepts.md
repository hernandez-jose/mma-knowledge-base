# Module 11 — The Concepts

> Techniques are the vocabulary. These are the grammar. A student who
> understands them learns every technique in the atlas roughly twice as fast.

## How to teach this module

**Don't teach it as a module.** Name one concept at the start of a class, use it
as the coaching cue all session, and let it explain the day's technique. Fourteen
concepts, fourteen weeks, then start again — they mean something different the
second time round.

## The two that deserve their own class slot

### Grip fighting

It isn't a "technique", so it never gets a slot — and it's the biggest single
skill gap between blue and purple belt. **Nobody sweeps you with grips you never
allowed.** Five minutes of [G-09](../drills/positional-games.md#g-09--grips-only) every
single class, standing and seated, gi and no-gi.

### The scramble

See [Module 10](10-scrambles.md). It decides more matches than any named position
and gets less training time than the berimbolo.

## Blending it into a roll

Concept tasks for [R-02](../drills/integration-rolls.md#r-02--task-roll) — each one turns
an abstraction into a scoreboard:

- "Name your range out loud every time it changes: free, grip, contact."
- "You may not grip anything until you've broken their grip first."
- "No muscle. If you feel yourself straining, let go and find the timing instead."
- "Every position you take, say the ladder number out loud."
- "Nasal breathing only. Mouth opens, round pauses."
- "After the round, explain one thing that failed — in concept terms, not technique terms."

---

## Checkpoint

- [ ] Explains a failed sweep in terms of base and posts rather than "I did it wrong"
- [ ] Wins grip exchanges against a same-level partner more often than not
- [ ] Frames instead of pushing when under pressure — observable from outside
- [ ] Keeps nasal breathing through a hard round
- [ ] Names the concept behind the day's technique without being prompted
