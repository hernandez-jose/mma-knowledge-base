## Why this module exists

Nobody quits jiu-jitsu because the techniques ran out. They quit because of a
knee, a neck, a burnout, an ego bruise, or a training schedule that never had a
rest week in it. Everything below is aimed at the same thing: **be able to train
again tomorrow, and in a decade.**

---

## 13.1 — Recovery

**Atlas:** [XIII Tier 9 — recovery](../../jujitsu.md#tier-9--off-the-mat-from-month-one-forever)

BJJ is high-frequency impact training. Treat it like one.

| Item | The standard |
|---|---|
| **Sleep** | 7–9 hours. The single most effective recovery intervention that exists, and the one everybody trades away first. |
| **Frequency** | 3–4 sessions a week is sustainable for most adults with jobs. Five-plus needs deliberate deload weeks. |
| **Deload** | One easy week every 6–8 weeks. Technique and flow rolling only. |
| **Hydration and fuel** | Train fed. A hungry roll is a sloppy roll, and sloppy rolls injure people. |
| **Rest days are training** | Adaptation happens between sessions, not during them. |

## 13.2 — Strength and conditioning that actually transfers

**Atlas:** [XIII Tier 9 — strength and conditioning](../../jujitsu.md#tier-9--off-the-mat-from-month-one-forever)

| Area | Why | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Neck** | Concussion and crank resilience. The highest-value prehab in the sport. | Isometrics four directions, banded work. [S-14](../drills/solo-drills.md#s-14--neck-grip-and-hip-prehab). |
| **Grip** | BJJ finger arthritis is real and near-universal. | Dead hangs, towel hangs, **finger extensor** work to balance all the gripping. Tape before it hurts, not after. |
| **Hips** | Every guard and every escape. | 90/90 switches, deep squat holds, couch stretch. |
| **Posterior chain** | Bridging, lifting, takedowns. | Hinges, carries, rows. |
| **Rotational core** | Every scramble and every sweep. | Chops, anti-rotation holds. |

Two sessions a week is enough. Lifting is not the sport — don't let it eat the
mat time.

## 13.3 — Injuries you can actually prevent

**Atlas:** [XV.b — long-term joint health](../../jujitsu.md#xvb--whole-domains-the-tables-above-under-serve) · [XIII Tier 0 — hygiene](../../jujitsu.md#tier-0--before-you-touch-anybody-first-10-minutes-day-one)

| Injury | Prevention |
|---|---|
| **Skin infections (staph, ringworm)** | Wash the gi after *every* session. Shower straight after. Cover open cuts. Don't train with an infection — one person ignoring this takes out a gym. |
| **Cauliflower ear** | Headgear, if you care about it. There is no other prevention. |
| **Finger and knuckle damage** | Tape prophylactically, and stop white-knuckling every grip. |
| **Neck** | [S-14](../drills/solo-drills.md#s-14--neck-grip-and-hip-prehab), plus no can openers or cranks, ever. |
| **Knees** | Tap to leg locks early. Don't drill throws on hard mats. Refuse the roll with the person who explodes into everything. |
| **The tweaks that become six months** | Say it out loud immediately and sit the round out. This is the whole intervention. |

## 13.4 — Note-taking

**Atlas:** [XIII Tier 9 — note-taking](../../jujitsu.md#tier-9--off-the-mat-from-month-one-forever)

Three lines after every class: **what worked, what didn't, what to ask about.**

It takes ninety seconds and roughly doubles retention. If you take one thing
from this entire curriculum, take this. See
[R-15](../drills/integration-rolls.md#r-15--debrief-roll).

## 13.5 — Video study

**Atlas:** [XIII Tier 9 — video study](../../jujitsu.md#tier-9--off-the-mat-from-month-one-forever)

Watch matches at **your weight and body type**, not highlight reels. A 60 kg
guard player learns nothing useful from a heavyweight pressure passer, however
beautiful it is. Watch a full match, pick one moment, and bring a question about
it to class.

## 13.6 — Choosing a game

**Atlas:** [XIII Tier 9 — choosing a game](../../jujitsu.md#tier-9--off-the-mat-from-month-one-forever) · [XV.b — gi vs no-gi](../../jujitsu.md#xvb--whole-domains-the-tables-above-under-serve)

By purple belt you need a preferred guard, a preferred pass and a preferred
finish — and honest reasons for each, based on your body rather than on whose
instructional you bought last.

| Question | Ask honestly |
|---|---|
| Am I usually the smaller one? | Then frames, timing, back attacks and speed passing scale; pressure passing won't. |
| Am I usually the bigger one? | Then pressure works — but don't let it hide a lack of technique that shows up the moment you meet somebody bigger. |
| Am I flexible? | Inversions and rubber-guard-style attacks open up. They also close as you age. |
| Am I over 40? | Slower entries, fewer explosive scrambles, more frames, more grips, earlier taps. |
| Gi or no-gi? | They are close to different sports. Split your study deliberately. |

## 13.7 — Adaptations the sport under-serves

**Atlas:** [XV.b — kids, older adults, women's considerations](../../jujitsu.md#xvb--whole-domains-the-tables-above-under-serve) · [XV.a — how to roll with different bodies](../../jujitsu.md#xva--things-missing-from-almost-every-syllabus)

| Group | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Over 40** | Nobody over 40 should be doing 100% rounds with 20-year-olds five times a week. Volume down, technique up, taps early. |
| **Kids** | Different injury profile, different pacing, restricted submissions. Play-based games over drilling. |
| **Women** | Strength-disparity strategy, self-defense priorities, and an emphasis on frames, timing and back attacks — which scale across strength gaps better than pressure passing. |
| **Returning from injury** | Announce it every round. Ask for positional work. The person who reinjures themselves is almost always the person who didn't say anything. |
| **Beginners in the room** | Slow down. You were them last year and you'll want them still training next year. |

## Checkpoint

- [ ] Three-line notes after every session for a month, unbroken
- [ ] Prehab circuit ([S-14](../drills/solo-drills.md#s-14--neck-grip-and-hip-prehab)) twice a week
- [ ] Gi washed every session, nails checked, cuts covered — without being told
- [ ] Took a full deload week in the last two months
- [ ] Can name a preferred guard, pass and finish, with a reason based on their body
- [ ] Has said "I need to sit this one out" at least once, without guilt
