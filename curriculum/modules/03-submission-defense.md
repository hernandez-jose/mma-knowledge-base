## Why this module exists

Two reasons, and the second one is the real one.

**Tactically:** you cannot attack freely until you're hard to finish. Defense is
what buys you the freedom to try things.

**For safety:** a white belt will meet a heel hook at an open mat years before
IBJJF says they may use one. Teaching leg lock *defense* first is not a tactical
choice, it's a protective one — and the same logic applies to the guillotine, the
armbar and the RNC, which are the three things every untrained person tries on
day one.

---

## The rule that organises the whole module

> **Early defense is a technique. Late defense is a prayer.**

Every attack has three stages, and the gap between them is enormous:

| Stage | What's happening | Your answer | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Early** | They're establishing the grip or the angle | Deny the grip, break the angle | Nothing — you stay neutral |
| **Middle** | Control is set, pressure isn't on | Posture, hand fight, structural escape | A position |
| **Late** | It's locked and tightening | Survival mechanics, then tap | A tap, or an injury if you're stubborn |

Almost all teaching happens at the *late* stage — because it looks dramatic.
Almost all of the value is at the *early* stage. Train early defense.

---

## What you must own

| Attack | Early defense | Middle | Never |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RNC** | Chin down before the hand arrives; shoulders shrugged | Two-on-one on the choking hand, peel and slide it over the head | Never let them get their bicep past your chin |
| **Triangle** | Posture up, one arm in one arm out — fix it immediately | Stack, grab your own far shoulder, walk the trapped arm across | Never leave both hands in |
| **Armbar** | Elbows tight to ribs, hands never crossing your centreline | Clasp your hands, turn the thumb up, stack and step over | Never rip straight back against a locked hip |
| **Guillotine** | Hand to your own throat before the chin passes | Shoulder pressure over the top, circle to the trapped-arm side, pop the head out | Never stand up into a high-elbow guillotine |
| **Kimura / americana** | Hide the wrist — grab your own hip or belt, or straighten the arm | Elbows to the ribs, turn *toward* the crank | Never let the arm float away from your body |
| **Straight ankle lock / leg entanglement** | Sit up, hide the heel, clear the knee line | Extract to the outside and come on top | Never spin away from a heel hook — tap |

## Lessons

| # | Lesson | Content | Game |
|:-:|---|---|---|
| **3.1** | Choke defense: RNC and guillotine | Chin, hands, and the hand fight that happens before either. | G-14, G-05 |
| **3.2** | Choke defense: triangle and arm triangle | Posture, the one-in-one-out rule, and the shoulder-walk escape from a locked arm triangle. | G-14 |
| **3.3** | Arm lock defense: armbar, kimura, americana | Elbows in, hide the wrist, turn toward the crank. Clasp early — never late. | G-14 |
| **3.4** | Leg lock defense, before any leg lock offense | Hide the heel, clear the knee line, extract. Eight weeks of defense only. | G-11, P-15 |
| **3.5** | When to tap, revisited | Joints: early, always. Compressions and cranks: they hurt before they're dangerous, but that's not permission to be brave. Heel hooks: there is no warning — tap on the *position*, not the pain. | — |

## The drill block

| Slot | Content |
|---|---|
| Warm-up | S-01, S-03, S-15 |
| Reps | Partner applies each attack at 30%, you defend at the **early** stage. 10 reps × 6 attacks. |

| Roll | [R-05 chain roll](../drills/integration-rolls.md#r-05--chain-roll) — catch-and-release means you get caught a lot, safely |

## Blending it into a roll

Tasks for [R-02](../drills/integration-rolls.md#r-02--task-roll):

- "Your partner attacks submissions only; you may not attack at all."
- "Every submission you escape, name the stage you escaped at — early, middle or late — out loud."
- "One round where your partner starts every restart with a submission 70% applied."
- "Count how many times your hands cross your own centreline. That's your armbar exposure."

---

## Checkpoint

- [ ] Early defense demonstrated to all six attacks at 70%
- [ ] Escapes the arm triangle and the triangle from a locked, non-pressurised position
- [ ] Extracts a leg from outside ashi without spinning
- [ ] Taps early on joints, without being reminded, in a live round
- [ ] Can state the difference between early, middle and late defense for any attack

---

## Safety

- Leg locks in this module are **defense only** until a coach says otherwise, and rotational attacks (heel hook, toe hold) are never applied at speed. See [`jujitsu.md` belt legality](../../jujitsu.md#legality-by-belt-ibjjf-adult).
- Every drill here has the attacker applying at a crawl. If a defense drill produces a real tap, it was run too fast.
- Neck cranks and can openers are not in this curriculum, at any level, in any drill.
