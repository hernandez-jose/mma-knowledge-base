# Module 04 — Guard and Retention

> Bottom is not losing. It's the only position in grappling where you can attack
> with all four limbs — and retention is the skill that keeps you there.

## Why this module exists

Two-thirds of everything you'll ever be taught from the bottom assumes you still
have a guard when the technique starts. Most people are taught twelve guards and
zero retention, then spend three years being passed before they get to try any
of them.

**Retention comes first here.** A student with one guard and real retention beats
a student with six guards and none.

---

## What you must own

| Guard | Priority | Why |
|---|:-:|---|
| **Closed guard** | 1 | Safest place to learn from. Breaks their posture, holds three submissions, and is the only guard that still works with strikes present. |
| **Half guard (knee shield)** | 1 | Where most people leak position. Also where an MMA fighter and a 50-year-old will spend most of their time. |
| **Butterfly / seated guard** | 2 | The engine of no-gi bottom play. Hooks, posture, elevator, arm drag. |
| **Basic open guard** (feet on hips, tripod, sickle) | 2 | Distance management, and the bridge between the others. |
| **Knee shield / z-guard** | 2 | The frame that makes half guard survivable. |
| **De la Riva / spider / lasso** | 3 | Excellent, grip-dependent, and gi-flavoured. Add once retention exists. |

Everything else in [Part II.b](../../jujitsu.md#iib--guards-bottom-positions-with-attacks)
is optional for years. Know it exists; don't build on it yet.

## Lessons

| # | Lesson | Content | Game |

| **4.1** | Retention before guards | Face them, frame, hip escape, leg pummel, re-guard. Your legs are arms — retention is a pummelling skill. | G-02 |

| **4.4** | Half guard bottom | Get on your side. Knee shield or underhook — never flat, never square. | G-15 |
| **4.5** | Butterfly and seated guard | Hooks in, posture up, chest connection, the arm drag as the twin of the elevator. | G-08 |
| **4.6** | Basic open guard | Feet on hips, tripod and sickle, and the distance rule: free, grip, or contact — pick one deliberately. | G-02 |
| **4.7** | Guard against a much bigger passer | Knee shield earlier, distance sooner, no flat backs, and the honest note that closed guard costs a big passer less than it costs you. | G-02 + [G-13](../drills/positional-games.md#g-13--handicap-games) |

## Blending it into a roll

Tasks for [R-02](../drills/integration-rolls.md#r-02--task-roll):

- "You may not close your guard at any point this round."
- "Every time you're passed, you have 15 seconds to recover before the round counts it against you."
- "Play only half guard. If you get to full guard, give it back."
- "Use exactly one guard for the whole round — pick it out loud first."
- "No submissions from the bottom: sweeps and retention only."

---

## Checkpoint

- [ ] Retains open guard against a 70% passer for 60 s without closing the guard
- [ ] Recovers guard from half guard bottom against a 70% pass attempt
- [ ] Breaks posture in closed guard and holds it against an actively posturing partner
- [ ] Gets to a knee shield within 5 seconds of landing in half guard, every time
- [ ] Names their preferred guard and gives an honest reason for it

---

## Common errors

| Error | Fix |
|---|---|
| Flat on the back, square to the passer | Face them. You should be on a hip roughly all the time. |
| Retaining with grips instead of hips | Grips buy a second; hips buy the position. Move first, grip second. |
| Playing four guards badly | Pick one guard for a month. Depth beats breadth. |
| Closed guard as a rest stop | Closed guard is an attacking position. If you're resting there, you're teaching yourself to stall. |
| Learning fancy guards before retention | You will never reach the fancy guard if you're passed on the way to it. |
