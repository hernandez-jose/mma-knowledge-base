## How to read the tables

Every move gets two numbers. They are the whole point of this document — a list of 300 techniques is useless without a sense of priority.

### Difficulty (`Diff`) — how hard the move is to actually pull off

| # | Level | What it means |
|:-:|---|---|
| **1** | Day one | A total beginner can be taught this in one class and use it that night. |
| **2** | Easy | A few weeks of drilling. Low coordination cost, forgiving of mistakes. |
| **3** | Moderate | Blue-belt territory. Needs timing, a setup, and a reaction to feed off. |
| **4** | Hard | Purple/brown. Long setup chains, tight details, or specific attributes (flexibility, explosiveness, grip strength). |
| **5** | Expert | Specialist move. High failure cost — miss it and you're in a worse spot than you started. |

### How common (`Com`) — how often it shows up in real training and competition

| # | Level | What it means |
|:-:|---|---|
| **5** | Everywhere | You will see or feel this every single session. Non-optional knowledge. |
| **4** | Very common | Core of most people's games. Expect it weekly. |
| **3** | Common | Regular sighting. Every gym has three people who love it. |
| **2** | Occasional | Situational or specialist. Shows up when conditions are right. |
| **1** | Rare | Niche, elite-only, or locked to one ruleset/uniform. Worth knowing exists. |

**A note on honesty:** the `Com` numbers are calibrated against competition data (IBJJF Worlds, ADCC, published match analyses) blended with what actually happens in a normal gym on a Tuesday. They are informed judgment, not a peer-reviewed dataset. `Diff` is pure coaching judgment. Your mileage will vary with body type, ruleset, and whether you wear a gi.

## Part I — The Map

Everything in jiu-jitsu is a fight to move up this ladder and stop your opponent moving up it. Learn this before you learn a single technique.

| Value | Position | Why it's worth that |
|:-:|---|---|
| **+4** | **Back control** | You can attack; they can't reach you. Highest finish rate in the sport. |
| **+3** | **Mount** | Full weight, both hands free, the whole upper-body submission menu. |
| **+2** | **Side control / knee-on-belly / north–south** | Pin them, tire them, harvest an arm or a neck. |
| **+1** | **Half guard top** | You're past the legs but not clear. Pressure and pass. |
| **0** | **Standing / open guard battle** | Neutral. Somebody is about to gain or lose a level. |
| **−1** | **Guard bottom** | Not losing. You have attacks — sweeps and submissions both. |
| **−2** | **Bottom side control / knee-on-belly** | Losing. Frame, breathe, shrimp. |
| **−3** | **Bottom mount** | Bad. Trap an arm and bridge before they mount high. |
| **−4** | **Back taken** | Worst. Two hands protect one neck; win the grip fight or lose. |

**The core rule of the sport:** *position before submission.* Climb the ladder, then finish. Reaching for a submission from a worse position usually costs you a rung.

## Part III — Takedowns

> **Reality check first.** In gi competition the most-used method of getting to the ground is *not* a takedown — it's the **guard pull**, by a margin of roughly 8:1 at black belt at the IBJJF Worlds. In no-gi and at ADCC the picture flips hard and wrestling dominates. Train takedowns anyway: they're the highest-leverage skill gap in most BJJ gyms, and the reason wrestlers do so well in the sport.

## Part V — Guard Passing

There are three families, and every pass is one of them:
- **Pressure passing** — kill their space, be heavy, grind through. Slow, safe, exhausting for them.
- **Speed passing** — move faster than their hips can reset. Fast, athletic, higher risk.
- **Leg-entanglement / smash passing** — control the legs directly so they never get to use them.

## Part X — The Concepts (more important than any move)

Techniques are the vocabulary. These are the grammar. A student who understands these learns every technique above twice as fast.

| Concept | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Position before submission** | Secure control, then attack. | The organising principle of the entire art. |
| **Frames** | Bone structures (forearms, shins) that hold space without muscular effort. | Frames are how small people survive big people. Muscle tires; skeleton doesn't. |
| **Hip escape (shrimping)** | Moving your hips away from pressure to create space. | Every bottom escape in the sport is built on it. |
| **Bridging** | Driving off your feet to lift and displace their weight. | The other half of every escape. |
| **Base** | Keeping your weight over your support points so you can't be tipped. | Whoever has base wins the sweep exchange. |
| **Posture** | Head up, spine straight, arms in. | Broken posture = broken defense. Almost every guard attack starts by breaking it. |
| **Connection / heaviness** | Applying your weight through their chest rather than through your own knees. | The difference between a 70kg person feeling light and feeling like a piano. |
| **Grip fighting** | Winning the exchange of hands before the exchange of technique. | The most under-trained skill in BJJ. Nobody sweeps you if you never let them grip. |
| **Distance management** | Three ranges: free, grip, and body contact. Choose which you're in. | Most bad positions come from being in the wrong range for your plan. |
| **The scramble** | The chaotic in-between where neither of you has position. | More matches are decided in scrambles than in "positions" — and almost nobody drills them. |
| **Timing over strength** | Acting on their movement rather than initiating against a set base. | The single biggest efficiency multiplier as you age. |
| **Chaining / the 2-on-1 problem** | Attacking so their defense to A opens B. | Nothing works in isolation above white belt. |
| **Breathing** | Nasal, rhythmic, and continuous — especially under pressure. | Panic is 80% oxygen debt. Fixing your breathing beats a year of cardio. |
| **The tap** | Voluntary, early, and without ego. | It is a *skill*, and the entire safety model of the sport rests on it. |

## Part XII — Rules and Rulesets (you need this before you compete)

### IBJJF points

| Action | Points | Notes |
|---|:-:|---|
| Takedown | **2** | Must land in guard or better and hold 3 seconds. |
| Sweep | **2** | Reverse from guard, stabilise 3 seconds. |
| Knee-on-belly | **2** | Held 3 seconds. |
| Guard pass | **3** | Clear the legs, establish control 3 seconds. |
| Mount | **4** | Held 3 seconds. |
| Back control (both hooks or body triangle) | **4** | Held 3 seconds. |
| **Advantage** | — | Awarded when a scoring action nearly happened. Breaks ties. |
| **Penalty** | — | Stalling, fleeing the mat, illegal grips. Four penalties = disqualification. |

### Legality by belt (IBJJF, adult)

| Technique | White | Blue | Purple | Brown | Black |
|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Straight ankle lock | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Wrist lock | ❌ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Kneebar | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Toe hold | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Calf / bicep slicer | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Heel hook (**no-gi only**) | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Heel hook (gi) | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| Knee reaping (**no-gi only**) | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Slams, neck cranks, spinal locks (twister) | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |

> Rules change. Check the current official ruleset for the specific event before you compete — this table reflects the post-2021 IBJJF position and is a study aid, not a legal document.

### How the major rulesets differ

| Ruleset | Character | Key difference |
|---|---|---|
| **IBJJF** | Points, time limits, gi and no-gi | Belt-based submission restrictions; guard pulling is legal and common. |
| **ADCC** | No-gi, two-period format | No points in the first half — encourages takedowns and finishes. Guard pulling is penalised. |
| **EBI / submission-only** | No points at all | Overtime rounds from set positions. Rewards finishers, punishes stallers. |
| **Combat Jiu-Jitsu** | Submission grappling with open-palm strikes | Bridges BJJ and MMA. |
| **Self-defense / MMA** | No rules, strikes present | Closed guard, posture control and the technical stand-up become far more important; guard pulling and inverted guard become far worse ideas. |

### Belt progression (rough reality)

| Belt | Typical time | What it actually means |
|---|---|---|
| White | 1–3 years | Survival. You are learning not to drown. |
| Blue | 2–3 years | You can escape and you have a game. IBJJF minimum: 2 years before purple. |
| Purple | 2–3 years | You're dangerous. Your game has a shape and a specialty. Minimum 1.5 years. |
| Brown | 1–2 years | Refinement and pressure. Minimum 1 year. |
| Black | 8–13 years total | The beginning, not the end. |

## Part XIII — The Syllabus: What Goes Into a Class

> This is the answer to *"what do I actually bring to class?"* — ordered by when a student needs it, not by how impressive it looks. Each tier assumes the one before it.
>
> Each tier below is expanded into a full module — lessons, drills, checkpoint — in the **[Curriculum](curriculum/modules/)**.

### Tier 0 — Before you touch anybody (first 10 minutes, day one)

| Item | Why it's first |
|---|---|
| **How to tap** — two clear taps on the body, or verbally | It's the safety system. Teach it before any submission exists. |
| **Mat etiquette** — bow/handshake, no shoes on the mat, ask before joining a roll, don't walk across other people's rolls | Culture is what keeps a room of people doing violence to each other safe. |
| **Hygiene** — gi washed after *every* session, nails cut short, shower before and after, cover open cuts, no jewellery | Staph and ringworm are the most common "injuries" in the sport, and long nails are the leading cause of non-combat injury. |
| **Don't train sick or infected** | One person ignoring this takes out a whole gym. |
| **Injury communication** — say it immediately, tell your partner about existing injuries *before* the round | Prevents the small tweak that becomes six months off. |

### Tier 1 — Movement (weeks 1–4, every single class)

Shrimp · reverse shrimp · bridge · technical stand-up · shoulder roll · breakfall · sprawl · pummelling · sit-through · penetration step.

### Tier 2 — Survive (weeks 1–8) — *the highest-value tier in the whole syllabus*

| Skill | Content |
|---|---|
| **Defensive posture** | Elbows in, hands protecting the neck, chin tucked, knees active. |
| **Frames** | Forearm and shin frames from under mount, side control and knee-on-belly. |
| **Mount escapes** | Upa, elbow-knee, heel drag. |
| **Side control escapes** | Frame-and-shrimp, underhook to dogfight, bridge-and-roll. |
| **Back defense** | Chin down, two-on-one hand fight, shoulder slide, hook removal. |
| **Turtle** | How to get there safely and how to get out (sit-out, stand up, granby). |
| **Submission defense** | Early RNC, triangle, armbar, guillotine and kimura defense. |

### Tier 3 — Guard (weeks 4–12)

| Skill | Content |
|---|---|
| **Closed guard** | Holding it, breaking posture, controlling grips, and the attack triangle: armbar / triangle / kimura. |
| **Half guard bottom** | Getting on your side, knee shield, underhook. *This is where most people leak position.* |
| **Guard retention** | Shrimping, framing, leg pummelling, re-guarding, hip mobility against a passer. |
| **Basic open guard** | Feet on hips, tripod, sickle, distance management. |
| **Butterfly / seated guard** | Hooks, posture, the elevator sweep, the arm drag. |

### Tier 4 — Sweep and reverse (weeks 8–16)

Scissor · hip bump · flower · lumberjack · kimura sweep · butterfly elevator · old school (half guard) · tripod/sickle · technical stand-up · wrestle-up.

### Tier 5 — Pass (weeks 8–20)

Opening the closed guard · knee cut · toreando · body lock pass · over-under · leg drag · smash pass · half guard passing (underhook + crossface) · headquarters as a hub · standing vs kneeling passing.

### Tier 6 — Attack (weeks 12+)

| Order | Content |
|---|---|
| **1. Chokes first** | RNC, cross collar, arm triangle, guillotine, triangle. Highest percentage, lowest injury risk. |
| **2. Then arms** | Armbar (from four positions), kimura, americana, omoplata. |
| **3. Then legs** | Straight ankle lock first, then entanglement positions (outside ashi, SLX, saddle), then rotational attacks *with a coach and slow*. |
| **4. Then chains** | Armbar↔triangle↔omoplata; kimura↔guillotine↔back; RNC↔collar choke↔armbar. |

### Tier 7 — Standing (introduce early, keep forever)

Stance and motion · grip fighting · collar tie · underhook and pummelling · penetration step · high single · double leg · ankle pick · snap down · body lock · osoto gari · ouchi gari · **sprawl and crossface** · front headlock · go-behind · the guard pull (done well, as a *choice*).

### Tier 8 — Ruleset and competition (before the first tournament)

Points and advantages · what's legal at your belt · match procedure · weigh-ins and gi checks · warming up at a venue · what stalling looks like to a referee · how to lose gracefully.

### Tier 9 — Off the mat (from month one, forever)

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| **Recovery** | Sleep, hydration, deload weeks, and the recognition that BJJ is high-frequency impact training. |
| **Strength and conditioning** | Neck training (concussion and crank resilience), grip work, hip mobility, posterior chain, and *rotational core* work. |
| **Nutrition and weight** | Fuelling training, and how to make weight without a crash cut. |
| **Note-taking** | Write three lines after every class: what worked, what didn't, what to ask about. This roughly doubles retention. |
| **Video study** | Watch matches at your weight and body type, not just highlight reels. |
| **Choosing a game** | By purple belt you need a preferred guard, a preferred pass and a preferred finish — and honest reasons for each. |

## Part XIV — Class Templates

### 60-minute class

| Minutes | Block |
|---|---|
| 0–8 | Warm-up: solo movements from Tier 1, specific to today's technique |
| 8–13 | Review of last class's technique (2 min explanation, 3 min drill) |
| 13–30 | New technique: 1–3 techniques max, taught in layers, drilled with increasing resistance |
| 30–42 | Positional sparring from today's exact position |
| 42–55 | Open rolling |
| 55–60 | Cool-down, questions, note-taking |

> **The most common coaching mistake is teaching too much.** Two techniques drilled to competence beat six demonstrated beautifully.

> Ready-made versions of this template, built from a single module's drill block, are in the **[Curriculum](curriculum/README.md#the-session-template)** — along with a 16-week cycle that schedules the whole atlas.


### 90-minute class

Add a 10-minute dedicated **standing block** and a 10-minute **escape block** before open rolling. These are the two things that get cut when time is short, and they are the two things students need most.

### Positional sparring — the highest-value 15 minutes of any class

Start from the exact position taught. Bottom person's goal: escape or sweep. Top person's goal: hold or advance. Reset every 90 seconds. This produces roughly three times the reps of the relevant situation compared with open rolling.

### XIV.a — Drilling and rolling formats

Positions and techniques are only half the atlas. The other half is *how you run
the round* — and a session plan that says "live rounds" with no format chosen is
the most common way training time gets wasted. Every format below is a real,
named thing you can put on a whiteboard.

**Intensity** is the number both partners agree on out loud before the round
starts. Most training injuries are two people rolling at different numbers.

| Format | What it is | Intensity | What it trains | Drill |
|---|---|:-:|---|---|
| **Cooperative drilling** | Partner gives the exact reaction, at half speed, so you can build the movement. | 0–20% | The shape of a technique | [P-series](curriculum/drills/partner-drills.md) |
| **Resistant drilling** | Same reps, but the partner defends with one named counter. | 40–60% | Timing and grip sequence | [P-series](curriculum/drills/partner-drills.md) |
| **Chain drilling** | Attack A → their named defense → attack B. Three links, no more. | 40–70% | Chaining; nothing works in isolation above white belt | [C-series](curriculum/drills/chain-drills.md) |
| **Catch-and-release** | Live rolling where a locked submission is released instead of finished. | 60–70% | Submission *entries*, safely and at volume | [P-13](curriculum/drills/partner-drills.md#p-13--catch-and-release-submissions) |
| **Positional sparring** | Live from one exact start position, with a win condition for each player, reset on a score or a clock. | 70–100% | Recognition — *when* a technique applies. **The highest-value 15 minutes of any class.** | [G-series](curriculum/drills/positional-games.md) |
| **King of the hill** | Winner stays in, fresh partner every round. | 80% | Volume, energy, and a scoreboard people care about | [R-08](curriculum/drills/integration-rolls.md#r-08--king-of-the-hill) |
| **Flow roll** | Continuous movement, no finishing, positions given up freely. Nobody wins. | 40–50% | Transitions, at three to four times the volume of a hard round | [R-01](curriculum/drills/integration-rolls.md#r-01--flow-roll) |
| **Task roll** | Normal roll, but each player has one stated objective for the round. | 70% | Getting today's technique into live rolling instead of losing it | [R-02](curriculum/drills/integration-rolls.md#r-02--task-roll) |
| **Handicap roll** | One player constrained — arm in the belt, defense only, starts in a bad spot. | 70–90% | A real fight for both people in a mismatched pair | [R-03](curriculum/drills/integration-rolls.md#r-03--handicap-roll) |
| **Positional restart roll** | Open rolling with a forced reset to a named position every 60–90 seconds. | 80% | Competition rhythm; kills stalling in weak positions | [R-04](curriculum/drills/integration-rolls.md#r-04--positional-restart-roll) |
| **Scramble / broken-position starts** | 20–30 second rounds from deliberately unstable positions, reset immediately. | 80% | The transitional chaos that decides most matches | [R-09](curriculum/drills/integration-rolls.md#r-09--broken-position-roll) |
| **Breathing roll** | Nasal breathing only; mouth opens, round pauses. | 60% | Composure. Panic is mostly oxygen debt. | [R-06](curriculum/drills/integration-rolls.md#r-06--breathing-roll) |
| **Scored roll** | A full match with a third person refereeing under a real ruleset. | 85% | Rules literacy — knowing *why* you won or lost | [R-11](curriculum/drills/integration-rolls.md#r-11--scored-roll) |
| **Shark tank** | One player stays in, fresh partners every 90 seconds. | 100% | Competition conditioning only. Peak weeks; never for beginners. | [R-07](curriculum/drills/integration-rolls.md#r-07--shark-tank) |
| **Strikes-aware / MMA round** | Grappling with open-palm contact or light gloves. | 60–80% | Which of your jiu-jitsu survives punches — see [training-plan.md](training-plan.md) | [R-13](curriculum/drills/integration-rolls.md#r-13--strikes-aware-roll) |
| **Ladder roll** | Submissions only count from +2 or better on the [positional ladder](#part-i--the-map). | 80% | *Position before submission*, with a scoreboard attached | [R-14](curriculum/drills/integration-rolls.md#r-14--ladder-roll) |
| **Body-mismatch protocol** | Named rules for rounds across a big size, age or skill gap — not a game, a safety standard. | varies | How to roll with a body unlike yours, which is most rounds | [R-12](curriculum/drills/integration-rolls.md#r-12--body-mismatch-roll) |
| **Blindfold start** | Eyes closed for the first 30 seconds from a ground start. | 60% | Reading weight and pressure instead of sight | [R-10](curriculum/drills/integration-rolls.md#r-10--blindfold-start) |
| **Debrief round** | Any format, plus three written lines afterwards: what worked, what didn't, what to ask. | any | Retention — roughly doubles what a session leaves behind | [R-15](curriculum/drills/integration-rolls.md#r-15--debrief-roll) |
| **Open rolling** | No constraints. | 70–100% | Nothing new — it's where you **audit** what you already own | [R-15](curriculum/drills/integration-rolls.md#r-15--debrief-roll) |

> **Open rolling is not where you learn.** Under real resistance you default to
> what you already own, which is exactly the material you need reps of least. New
> techniques survive only if a format above forces them into the round first —
> that's what the [curriculum's integration rolls](curriculum/drills/integration-rolls.md)
> are for.

## Part XV — Gap Analysis: What The List Above Still Misses

> The instruction was to build the list, then look at it honestly and find the holes. Here they are. Most "complete" BJJ curricula — including the one above, in its first draft — leave out the same things, and they're rarely the techniques.

### XV.a — Things missing from almost every syllabus

| Gap | Why it gets left out | Why it matters | When to add it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Grip fighting as its own subject** | It isn't a "technique," so it never gets a class slot. | You cannot be swept or passed by grips you never allowed. It is the standing and seated equivalent of frames, and it's the biggest single skill gap between blue and purple belt. | Week 2, then five minutes every class forever. |
| **The scramble** | It's chaotic, so it can't be demonstrated cleanly. | More matches are decided in the transitional chaos than in named positions. Nobody trains the thing that decides most matches. | Positional rounds starting from deliberately broken positions. |
| **Turtle — offensively and defensively** | It looks like losing, so gyms skip it. | Everyone ends up there. Untrained, it's a free back-take donation for your entire white-belt career. | Tier 2, alongside escapes. |
| **The front headlock system** | It comes from wrestling, so BJJ gyms treat it as optional. | It's the hub connecting takedown defense, guillotines, D'Arces, anacondas and back takes. Enormous return on investment. | Tier 7, but genuinely useful from day 30. |
| **Wrestling up from bottom** | BJJ culture assumes bottom means guard, not standing. | The highest-scoring bottom action in modern no-gi. It's also the self-defense answer: get up. | Tier 4. |
| **Breakfalls** | Gi gyms that don't throw don't teach falling. | The single best injury-prevention skill in the sport, and it transfers to life outside the gym. | Tier 1, week one. |
| **Breathing under pressure** | Considered soft or unteachable. | Panic is mostly oxygen debt. Teaching nasal breathing under a heavy top player fixes more "cardio problems" than conditioning does. | Tier 0, revisited constantly. |
| **How to roll with different bodies** | Assumed to be obvious. | How to roll with someone 30kg heavier, 20 years older, brand new, or twice your skill — each requires a different approach, and getting it wrong is how people get hurt and quit. | First month. |
| **Leg lock *defense* before leg lock offense** | Offense is more fun to teach. | Beginners meet heel hooks in open mats long before their belt allows them. Teaching defense first is a safety measure, not a tactical one. | Tier 2, defense only. |
| **When *not* to tap vs when to tap early** | Uncomfortable to discuss. | Nuance: tap early always for joints; understand that neck cranks and compressions can hurt before they're dangerous — but never gamble on a heel hook. | Ongoing. |
| **Ego and losing** | Feels like therapy, not coaching. | The number one reason people quit in the first six months, and the number one cause of injury in the first two years. | Explicitly, week one. |

### XV.b — Whole domains the tables above under-serve

| Domain | What's missing | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-defense vs sport** | Punch-block posture in closed guard, standing in base, headlock defense, wall/ground escapes against strikes, distance management against a striker. | Sport BJJ has drifted a long way from this. Inverted guard, berimbolo and 50/50 are excellent sport positions and terrible fight positions. If self-defense is your goal, the syllabus reorders significantly. |
| **MMA-specific grappling** | Cage wrestling, ground-and-pound defense, posture control from bottom, wall walks, striking-aware guard selection. | See **[training-plan.md](training-plan.md)** — that's the file that covers this properly. |
| **Gi vs no-gi as different sports** | Grip-dependent guards (spider, lasso, worm) evaporate without cloth; head-and-arm control, wrestling and leg locks matter far more without it. Sweat changes everything. | Decide which one you're training for and split your study accordingly. |
| **Kids' and older-adult adaptations** | Different injury profiles, different pacing, different submission restrictions. | Nobody over 40 should be doing 100% rounds with 20-year-olds five times a week. |
| **Women's-specific considerations** | Weight and strength disparity strategy, self-defense priorities, closed-guard and back-control emphasis. | Frames, timing and back attacks scale better across strength gaps than pressure passing does. |
| **Long-term joint health** | Neck training, finger and grip care (BJJ finger arthritis is real and near-universal), cauliflower ear prevention, knee care. | Tape your fingers before they hurt, not after. Headgear if you value your ears. |
| **Refereeing and rules literacy** | Most competitors don't know why they lost. | Learn to read your own match. |
| **Coaching and teaching skill** | Being good at BJJ and being able to teach it are unrelated abilities. | Brown belts should be teaching. |

### XV.c — Techniques the tables omitted on purpose

For completeness, these exist and were deliberately left out of the main tables as too niche, too dangerous, or ruleset-banned. Know they exist; don't build a game on them yet.

| Move | Status | Image / source |
|---|---|---|
| **Slams / spiking** | Banned in almost every ruleset. Can cause catastrophic spinal injury. | [Illegal Slam vs. Legit Takedown – BJJEE](https://www.bjjee.com/articles/understanding-the-difference-between-an-illegal-slam-a-legit-takedown/) |
| **Heel hooks in the gi** | Banned by IBJJF at every belt. Legal in some other rulesets. | [The New IBJJF Rules For Heel Hooks And Leg Reaping – FloGrappling](https://www.flograppling.com/articles/6856722-the-new-ibjjf-rules-for-heel-hooks-and-leg-reaping-in-2021) |
| **Twister, spinal locks, neck cranks** | Banned by IBJJF. Legal in some no-gi and MMA rulesets. | [BJJ 101: Twister – Evolve Daily](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/bjj-101-twister/) |
| **Small-joint manipulation (fingers)** | Banned everywhere. | [Small joint manipulation – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_joint_manipulation) |
| **Suplexes** | Legal in some rulesets, but rarely worth the risk on mats. | [Suplex – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suplex) |
| **Scissor takedown (kani basami)** | Banned by IBJJF and IJF — has broken legs. | [Kani Basami — Judo Technique – After The Mat](https://afterthemat.com/library/judo/kani-basami) |

### XV.d — The honest final gap

The largest omission in any list of techniques is that **the list is not the skill.** A student who genuinely owns fifteen of these moves — the upa, the frame-and-shrimp, the back escape, the closed guard, the knee shield, the knee cut, the toreando, the scissor sweep, the butterfly sweep, the RNC, the armbar, the triangle, the arm triangle, the guillotine, the straight ankle lock — will beat a student who can name three hundred.

**Depth beats breadth. Escapes beat submissions. Chokes beat joint locks. Position beats everything.**

Use this file to know what exists and to decide what to ignore. Use the mat to actually get good.
